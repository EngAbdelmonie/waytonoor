Doorway: The Idol-Worshipper / Traditional Polytheist — Doorway Layer (D11)

Doorway Data and Its Bundle (per the Materials and Tools Specification)

  • Project: WayToNoor — «طريق النور» (identity lockup B7).
  • Doorway layer: One of nine doorway layers (B8) fitted onto the single approved core. Naming warning: The name of this slot is an internal classification label only and never goes out to the visitor; in every interface the person is addressed by his own name, his community, and the religion of his family, exactly as he names himself.
  • Lead unit: 2 — Allah, the One, phrased as "the Supreme Creator you already know."
  • Build priority (Specification 5.10): Medium — good, quiet service with one well-crafted path.
  • State of the field — a documented near-total gap: There are no modern da'wah materials dedicated to this group as an audience in its own right: where it is mentioned at all, it is forcibly merged with the Hindu on a single page, or treated historically (the polytheists of Quraysh in tafsir and sirah) or juridically (classification of rulings). The group exists in the Shariah classification and is absent from contemporary da'wah products. This doorway was built from academic studies of traditional religions + the documented Rassoul pattern + our core.
  • Doorway bundle: The text of this doorway + an alternative opening video for Unit 2 (oral in character first — see the P1 calibration) + audio + posts + interactive + Companion tool + a context layer from the Calibration Document (the P1 file is this doorway's first environment).
  • Binding rules (D12/D13): The rule of not reviling is a moral bridge before any content: "And do not insult those they invoke other than Allah, lest they insult Allah in enmity without knowledge" [Al-An'am: 108]; no rhetorical smashing of symbols; no pushing for a sudden public decision that would break him socially; building an alternative social network is a condition parallel to the content, not subsequent to it.
  • Status of the piece: A doorway layer produced after approval of the core (2026-11-26) — pending religious and linguistic review and the project owner's approval. Not for publication in this phase.

1. Who Is This Visitor? And What Does He Actually Believe?

This visitor — a traditional polytheist, or a follower of indigenous/tribal religions, or one whose spiritual life revolves around ancestral spirits, forces of nature, and sacred places — usually does not come to us with a question; the call comes to him, through a Companion, a community, or content passing through his village and his market. His spiritual world, as studies of traditional religions describe it (foremost among them John Mbiti's work on African religions and philosophy): a living cosmos inhabited by spiritual forces present in nature, the ancestors, and places; and tangible, mediated worship (an idol / tree / grave / shrine) which for many is a channel, not an end — a means of drawing near to higher powers; and a tribal and family identity fused into ritual so completely that "religion" and "we" are almost one word.

And the structural truth on which this entire doorway stands — documented in the academic studies of African and other traditional religions: behind the intermediaries, in most of these systems, stands a supreme Creator God (under many local names: Olodumare among the Yoruba, Nyame among the Akan, Chukwu among the Igbo — documented examples in the literature of the field), whose direct, daily worship was largely left aside because He is "far away / high above," while the intermediaries took over daily affairs. And this meets — with striking precision — what the Qur'an itself describes about the first Arab polytheists: that when they were asked who created the heavens and the earth, they acknowledged Allah: "And if you asked them, 'Who created the heavens and earth?' they would surely say, 'Allah'" [Luqman: 25], and that they made their intermediaries "intercessors" and "bringers-near," not creators. So our doorway does not begin by nullifying his intermediaries; it begins from his own acknowledgement of the Supreme Creator.

His first practical fear: the anger of the spirits/ancestors if he leaves, and the severing of his bond with the community if he declares. His implicit question: "Your distant, absent God… against my present powers whose effect I can touch — why would I exchange?"

2. Entry Questions and Hooks

  • "We have known the Supreme Creator since our fathers; our intermediaries only bring us near to Him"
  • "The religion of my fathers and my ancestors; leaving it is betrayal of them"
  • "Every community has its path, its powers, and its land — why your path?"

The alternative opening for the lead unit (2) — its proposed verbatim text (in oral phrasing first, to be told before it is read):

"You already believe that above all powers there is a Supreme Creator who created the heaven and the earth — your fathers knew Him and named Him in the name of your language. This unit does not bring you a strange god; it asks you one question, with respect: this Supreme Creator whom you know… is He really far away, as you were told? Or is He nearer to you than every intermediary — hearing you, yourself, directly, with no carved stone and no middleman? Come, let us hear what He says about Himself."

3. Bridges to Islam

  1. His own acknowledgement of a Supreme Creator behind the intermediaries — the first and most important bridge in this entire doorway (a documented pattern in Rassoul's address to this group, and supported by the Qur'an's own description of the belief of the first polytheists).
  2. The order and unity of creation: seasons, rain, and crops moving by one system across all the earth — a quiet argument for the oneness of the One who governs, in the language of his agricultural world, not the language of the laboratory.
  3. Respect for righteous ancestors is redirected, not insulted: honouring, remembering, and praying for righteous dead has lawful forms with us (dutifulness, remembrance, and supplication) — without worship. The difference between honouring and worship is the heart of the dialogue, and it is presented with patience.
  4. The rule of not reviling: we do not revile what he holds sacred and we do not insult his symbols — conduct that builds trust before any content, and for this visitor is evidence of sincerity prior to any argument.
  5. A present alternative community: a mosque, a community, and a Companion from his region — because what he loses socially must find its living counterpart, not a promise.

4. Objections in Their Strongest Form — And Where They Are Answered in the Core

The objection in its strongest form Our pattern of answer Its place in the core
"These are our intercessors; they only bring us near" A graduated question about the intermediary, without condemnation: Does he hear you? Does he possess anything for himself or for you? And why an intermediary made by your own hand between you and the One who is nearer to you than he is? — Questions he answers himself across the sessions, not rulings dictated to him Unit 2 (Allah, the Near, the All-Hearing) + Unit 11
"The religion of my fathers; leaving it is betrayal" Separating loyalty to family (dutifulness, kindness, and continuing remembrance — not cut off by Islam but confirmed by it) from following them in belief; and truth is not inherited, just as satiety is not inherited from hunger Unit 3 + Unit 9 (dutifulness to parents and upholding ties of kinship)
"Every community has its gods and its land" One Creator for the whole earth, against local gods that do not even claim universality for themselves; and messengers from every nation — one of them may have been from his own people in ancient times Unit 4 (messengers from every nation) + Unit 2
Fear of punishment from the spirits/ancestors if he leaves Treatment of a fear, not an argument: gradual reassurance, a present Companion, and an alternative community standing before any declaration, and no pushing for a sudden public decision that would break him socially — his rhythm is what decides the timing Companion Card + Journey Level 1 (a flexible rhythm with no penalty for interruption — D6)

5. The Path: The Lead Unit and the Order of Presentation

The complete order for this doorway: 2 ← 3 ← 4 ← 1 ← 8 ← 6 ← 5 ← 7 ← 9 ← 11 ← 10 ← 12 last, and deliberately delayed (his decision is socially explosive; Unit 12 is not presented to him until his alternative network is standing).

  • Emphasis: Directness without intermediaries as glad tidings of liberation ("He hears you and you hear Him, with no middleman"); and the alternative community's generosity.
  • De-emphasis: Rhetorical smashing of symbols; public haste; any relief/material promise we do not possess (see the P1 calibration: the service-comparison question is answered honestly).
  • The one next step on his entry (B12): "The Supreme Creator you already know" — Unit 2 with this doorway's opening, orally/by audio first.
