1. Who Is This Visitor? And What Does He Actually Believe?

Zoroastrianism is among the oldest living monotheistic religions. Its founder is Zoroaster (Zarathustra), and the oldest texts of his religion are attributed to him: the Gathas — hymns in the Avesta regarded as his own words. His creed, as its people and its scholars describe it: one wise Creator God, Ahura Mazda (the Wise Lord); and a cosmic order standing on Asha — truth, order, and uprightness — against Druj — falsehood and chaos; and a human being who is free and choosing, standing as a conscious soldier in this struggle by his own choice; and a threefold ethic that summarises the whole religion: good thought, good word, and good deed (Humata, Hukhta, Hvarshta). And after death: an individual reckoning, and a crossing over a bridge (Chinvat) separating the righteous from the wicked, and a Paradise and a Fire, and at the end of time a final renewal of the world (Frashokereti) in which good triumphs completely.

And the point of precision on which this doorway stands: in the oldest texts (the Gathas) monotheism appears at its purest — Ahura Mazda is Creator of everything, and "the two twin spirits" (the good and the evil, Yasna 30) are created/chosen at the origin of the moral struggle; whereas hard cosmic dualism (an eternal power of evil, quasi-independent, a rival to the Creator) developed in later ages and reached extreme forms in late schools (such as Zurvanism). So the real place of disagreement between us and this visitor is not "Is your God one?" — but: Is evil an eternal, rival power, or a created thing permitted in a world of trial? A lofty theological question between two monotheisms, to be conducted at this level or not conducted at all.

His community today is small and relatively closed: academic estimates run around one hundred to two hundred thousand people globally (the Parsis in India — about 57,000 in the 2011 Census of India — and the Zoroastrians of Iran and the diaspora). His religion does not usually proselytise, nor is it proselytised; so if our visitor arrives, he arrives as an individual seeker, usually from a purely intellectual motive.

2. Entry Questions and Hooks

  • "Our religion is the oldest living monotheism; are we not the origin and you the branch?"
  • "What is your position on Zoroaster himself — is he not a possible prophet in your logic?" (his most important question for us)
  • "You call us in your books 'Magians' and describe us wrongly as worshipping fire — why should I trust the rest of your description?"

The alternative opening for the lead unit (4) — its proposed verbatim text:

"Thousands of years ago, a man stood in the steppes of ancient Iran asking your very question: Who is behind this cosmic order? And he answered: One wise Lord, and a struggle between truth and falsehood, and a free human being who chooses. His name with you is Zoroaster. And our Book says: 'And We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, [saying], "Worship Allah"' [An-Nahl: 36] — into every nation, without exception. This unit does not ask you whether Zoroaster was a prophet; it asks you to look with us at the whole chain: What if his voice was one of its first voices, and the seal came to purify the origin, not to abolish it?"

And preceding any dialogue — at the first confusion — is the explicit classificatory apology for the description of fire worship: precision here is trust, and acknowledging the common error is the first bridge.

3. Bridges to Islam

  1. One Creator God at the origin: His original monotheism in the Gathas — the bridge is historical–monotheistic: "Your origin is an ancient monotheism, and we call to its purification, not to something strange to it."
  2. Zoroaster within the framework of "a messenger for every nation": The possibility that he was among Allah's messengers before his religion changed over the centuries is presented as a respected possibility, not a settled fact — and the Qur'an itself mentions the Magians in the context of the nations between whom Allah will judge on the Day of Resurrection [Al-Hajj: 17] without naming Zoroaster; so we do not claim what has not been established.
  3. The threefold ethic: Good thought/word/deed — matching almost a complete Islamic moral system (Unit 9 is a ready presentation bridge).
  4. Individual reckoning and the Hereafter: The Chinvat bridge, the reckoning, Paradise, and the Fire — he has a complete Hereafter system, so Unit 8 with him is confirmation, not foundation.
  5. Human freedom and responsibility: Man is choosing, not compelled — common ground in the question of decree and evil.
