1. Who Is This Visitor? And What Does He Actually Believe?

The Christian visitor is not a blank page where faith is concerned: he already believes in one Creator God, in revelation and prophets, in a Day of Reckoning, and that God has speech He sent down. His opening question is not "Does God exist?" but three connected questions: Who is Jesus? Is Muhammad ﷺ a messenger from God? And is the Qur'an the speech of God?

His belief, as its own adherents describe it — not as its opponents draw it — rests on this: that God is one in His essence, existing in three Persons (the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit) — which for them is monotheism, not a plurality of gods, and they insist on that explicitly in their creed (Nicaea, 325 CE and after). That humanity inherits Adam's original sin, and that God, in His love, sent His Son Jesus Christ incarnate, who died crucified as a ransom for the sins of humanity and rose on the third day, and that salvation is by faith in him and by His grace. And that the Bible (in both its Testaments) is the inspired speech of God. The denominations vary in real details that must be respected in how they are addressed: Catholics and Orthodox honour Mary, peace be upon her, with high honour and believe in the intercession of the saints; Protestants focus on Scripture alone and grace alone; and the Eastern Orthodox (among them the Christians of the Arab world) have an ancient liturgical tradition. One doorway with three internal colours — and denominational calibration inside the doorway is part of this doorway's craft, not a luxury.

His highest emotional sensitivity: the place of Jesus and Mary, peace be upon them both. Any insult — real or imagined — to either of them closes the door at once, and usually for good. His second sensitivity: being treated as ignorant of his own religion; many visitors to this doorway know their Book well.

2. Entry Questions and Hooks

His questions, as he actually asks them: - "You love Jesus too? What exactly does your Qur'an say about him?" - "If God is one for you and for us, where is the real difference?" - "I am saved by the grace of Christ; what does Islam add for me?" - "Do you believe the Bible has been altered?" (He asks it defensively, early, and anxiously.) - "Why did a prophet come after Jesus at all?"

The alternative opening for the lead unit (4) — its proposed verbatim text:

"Why do Muslims name their sons Isa and their daughters Maryam? Why is there a whole surah in the Qur'an named Maryam (Mary), and another named Aal-e-Imran — the family of Mary? And why does the Qur'an describe Jesus as the Word of Allah and a spirit from Him, born of a virgin, who gave life to the dead and healed the blind and the leper by Allah's permission? This unit does not begin from the disagreement between us; it begins from the one story we together believe God wrote the first chapters of: from Adam to Abraham to Moses to Jesus… and then we ask together, honestly: did the story end there?"

The "word in common" rule for the opening: The conversation always opens from the common ground declared in the Qur'an itself: "Say, 'O People of the Scripture, come to a word that is equitable between us and you — that we will not worship except Allah and not associate anything with Him and not take one another as lords instead of Allah'" [Aal-e-Imran: 64]. Beginning from common ground is not politeness; it is the structure of this doorway.

3. Bridges to Islam

  1. Love of Jesus and Mary within Islam itself: A Muslim does not merely believe in the prophethood of Isa (Jesus), peace be upon him — his faith is not sound without it; and the Qur'an honours Mary with an honour unmatched in any book (a surah bearing her name, and her being chosen above the women of the worlds [Aal-e-Imran: 42]: "indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of the worlds" — Saheeh International wording for the sense of the verse). This bridge is emotional before it is doctrinal — especially for Catholics and Orthodox.
  2. The one God of Abraham: The story begins with the very prophet both parties trace themselves back to; Islam presents itself as the completion of a story the visitor knows, not a story strange to him.
  3. The shared chain of prophets: Names he knows and loves (Abraham, Moses, David, Zechariah, John…) are venerated in the Qur'an as his prophets too.
  4. Close ethics and practices: Prayer, fasting, charity, modest dress, and love of neighbour — shared lived ground that softens the strangeness of "the other religion."
  5. Allah, "the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate": Correcting the image of "the harsh God of the Muslims" from the heart of Unit 2 — Allah's love and mercy are a shared door (for them, in meaning, "God is love"; for us, almost every surah opens with mercy).

4. Objections in Their Strongest Form — And Where They Are Answered in the Core

The objection is presented here as its most intelligent holders state it (Enrichment Principle 10); the full answer lives in the core — the doorway points to it and does not repeat it.

The objection in its strongest form Our pattern of answer Its place in the core
The Trinity: "We are monotheists: one essence and three Persons, like the sun, its disc, and its ray; describing us as polytheists is a misunderstanding" We present their understanding accurately first, then ask the question of coherence calmly: are the Persons really distinct, or are they attributes? And we connect it to the simplicity of Qur'anic monotheism, without mockery and without pinning on them a plurality they do not claim Unit 2 (Al-Ikhlas: "He neither begets nor is born" [Al-Ikhlas: 3]) + Unit 4 (the place of Isa) + Unit 11 (the Trinity file)
Atonement and original sin: "God is just, so He does not forgive without a price, and merciful, so He paid the price Himself; your justice without atonement means sin is simply forgotten for free" We present the alternative as good news, not as polemic: fair personal responsibility — "And no bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another" [Fatir: 18] — and direct forgiveness through sincere repentance, with no intermediary — then an explicit comparative question: which of the two pictures is more just and more merciful? Unit 2 (Allah, the Most Forgiving, the Most Merciful) + Unit 8 (repentance within the pillars of faith) + Unit 11
The crucifixion: "The crucifixion and the empty tomb are among the best-documented events of ancient history for historians; your denial of it is a denial of history" A separate historical/textual file, late in the path, not an opening gate; the Qur'anic position is presented calmly, with the clarification that the axis of Islam is monotheism, not this event alone Unit 4 (the Qur'an's account of Isa) + Unit 11 (the crucifixion file)
"The Qur'an was copied / borrowed from the Bible" The file of the text's preservation and its history is an optional, in-depth path for whoever asks for it; the histories of the two texts are compared with the same historical tools both parties accept Unit 6 (preservation of the Qur'an) + Unit 11
"Why a prophet after Jesus?" From within his own logic: the chain did not stop at a single prophet in his own Book either; Unit 4 presents the chain, and Unit 5 presents the claim of the Seal and its evidence Units 4 and 5

5. The Path: The Lead Unit and the Presentation Order

The full order for this doorway: 4 ← 5 ← 2 ← 6 ← 8 ← 3 ← 7 ← 9 ← 1 ← 10 ← 11 ← 12.

  • Emphasis: The continuity of the one story ("not a new, disconnected religion, but a completion"); and Mary as a special emotional bridge for Catholics and Orthodox, brought forward early inside Unit 4.
  • De-emphasis: Opening the file of the alteration of the texts early (forbidden — D12); the language of "unbelievers" in the interface; any mockery of the cross or of rituals.
  • The one next step when he enters through this doorway (B12): "Read the story of Isa (Jesus), peace be upon him, as the Qur'an tells it" — Unit 4 with this doorway's opening.
