"Your First 30 Days" — The Ladder to the Light, First Rung: The Testimony of Faith and Your First Week

WayToNoor — «طريق النور» — "Your way to the Light of Islam — with a companion for your first steps."

Executive Summary and Prerequisites

This level is your home in your first week: it settles you, emotionally and in knowledge, after you have pronounced the two testimonies — what exactly happened, the meaning of what you pronounced, in depth, the natural feelings of the first week, the first things about purification, a wise plan for the people around you, and three mistakes to avoid. The only prerequisite: that you have pronounced the two testimonies (Unit 12); for this level is the content of the "First 7 Days Bundle" that was opened for you the moment you pronounced them.

Level Objectives

By the end of this rung you will be able to: - Explain in your own words what changed in your life on the day you pronounced the two testimonies, and the meaning of each testimony, in depth. - Deal with the feelings of the first week (elation, doubt, longing, loneliness) with practical tools, not by suppressing them. - Perform the bath of entering Islam and know the first things about general purification and the acts of the fitrah (the natural disposition). - Set down a wise written plan for telling your family and the people around you, and avoid the three mistakes of the first week.

Lesson 1: Your First Day as a Muslim — What Exactly Happened?

Lesson objective: That the truth of what took place settles in your heart: a completely new page with Allah, and a covenant that begins, not an exam that ends.

You woke up this morning a different person, even if everything around you looks the same: the same room, the same phone, the same faces. What changed is your relationship with your Creator: you pronounced the two testimonies with understanding and willing acceptance, so Allah erased all the sins that were before them, as the Prophet ﷺ said to 'Amr ibn al-'As: "Do you not know that Islam wipes out what came before it?" — narrated by Muslim (121). Today, in your account with Allah, you are as on the day your mother gave birth to you; so do not carry into your new life the burdens of regret over a page that has been closed — carry instead the gratitude of someone for whom a blank page has been opened. And in this first week a welcome worthy of your decision awaits you: an online celebration gathering that brings you together with the founder and with scholars and teachers who congratulate you and answer your questions, and you may receive a motivational celebration video made personally for you — it is yours alone, and nothing of it is published except with your explicit consent.

And what does it mean, practically, that you are a "Muslim" now? It means you have entered into a covenant with Allah: to worship Him alone, to learn your religion rung by rung, and to belong to a vast nation stretching across continents and centuries. It does not mean you have become infallible from tomorrow, nor that you are required to be a "complete Muslim" within a week; perfection is the journey of a whole lifetime, and today you are on the first, sound rung of your ladder.

Sources: Sahih Muslim (121) — the hadith "Islam wipes out what came before it" — verified via Dorar al-Sunniyah (dorar.net). The Noble Qur'an: [Az-Zumar: 53]. IslamWeb website — materials accompanying the new Muslim on his first day.

Performed practice task — “Take the step”

Before you sleep tonight, write down honestly: what feeling dominates you most right now — joy, relief, or fear of what is coming? One line is enough; for each of these feelings there is a lesson ahead in this level.

Companion Card (short): He reads the feeling line before the first meeting and starts from it; if the dominant feeling is fear, the feelings lesson (3) is brought forward in the week's plan.

Lesson 2: The Two Testimonies Again — A Deeper Understanding of What You Pronounced

Lesson objective: That what you pronounced turns from a memorised sentence into a settled meaning that you phrase in your own words.

Go back in your heart to the two phrases that changed your life: "I testify that there is no god but Allah, and I testify that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." The first testimony negates and affirms in a single breath: it negates divinity and worship from everything other than Allah — whether an idol, a desire, a human being or money — and affirms them for Allah alone. That is why, in the days ahead, you will feel small, deep shifts: a supplication you did not know rising from your heart at a moment of fear, an inner holding back from habits you used to do without thinking, and a new desire to know more.

And the second testimony is a covenant of following: to make Muhammad ﷺ your practical guide to Allah; so whenever you ask in the coming days, "How do I pray? How do I treat my family? How do I repent?" your ready answer is: how did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ do it? And here is an immediate practical benefit: you do not have to invent your religion yourself, nor imitate any Muslim you happen to meet; you have a documented measure: a preserved Qur'an and a carefully transmitted Sunnah, and between them trustworthy scholars whom you ask about whatever is unclear to you, through the approved sources on this site.

Sources: The Noble Qur'an: [Muhammad: 19], [An-Nisa: 80]. Islam Question & Answer website — "The meaning of the two testimonies and their conditions," in definitional wording. IslamWeb website — materials on "There is no god but Allah: its meaning and its pillars."

Performed practice task — “Take the step”

Pronounce the two testimonies again, slowly, then write by hand (or in the app's notebook), under each part of them, its meaning in your own words, not ours, and keep the paper; for a meaning you phrase yourself settles deeper in your heart.

Companion Card (short): He looks at the meanings paper if the learner shares it; any confusion in understanding the negation and the affirmation is corrected at the meeting, gently, with an example from the learner's own life.

Lesson 3: Feelings After the Decision — All of Them Are Natural, So Do Not Fear Them

Lesson objective: That you are armed with a correct interpretation of the first week's feelings, so that a passing wave does not shake you.

A scenario that repeats with many new Muslims in the first week: overwhelming elation on the day of the testimony, then on the third day a sudden wave of doubt: "Did I rush? What if I was wrong?" And on the fifth day a strange longing for old habits, and on the seventh a feeling of loneliness, because everyone around you has not changed along with you. Hear it from us clearly: all of these feelings are natural and expected, and many who passed through them before you became, afterwards, among the most steadfast of people. For the great decisions in a person's life — marriage, emigration, a change of career — are always followed by a brief psychological tremor; so how about the greatest decision of an entire lifetime?

The treatment for doubt is not suppressing it or being ashamed of it, but laying it before its causes: Is it a genuine scholarly question? Then research and ask until you are at rest — and the door of questions with us is open with no ceiling. Or is it a social fear of what people will say? Then it is a problem of surroundings, not a problem of conviction, and it has its lesson — the fifth in this level. Or is it a passing whisper (waswasah)? Then know that the Prophet ﷺ reassured his Companions when they complained to him of whispers touching faith itself, telling them that their very hatred of them and sense of their gravity is a sign of pure faith — as in Sahih Muslim (132), from the hadith of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him.

Sources: Sahih Muslim (132) — the hadith "That is pure faith," concerning whispering — verified via Dorar al-Sunniyah (dorar.net). IslamWeb website — materials on "The psychology of the new Muslim and whispers after entering Islam." Islam Question & Answer website — answers for firming up new Muslims.

Performed practice task — “Take the step”

If a wave of doubt comes to you this week, write its question down literally and send it through the questions channel or to your companion — do not leave it to grow in the dark; for a written question is half the answer. (And if no wave comes, write the question you most expect to visit you.)

Companion Card (short): A sent doubt question is answered by an announced time and is not left hanging; if the waves repeat intensely, or symptoms of depression accompany them, the psychological referral protocol is activated (the level's fixed rule).

Lesson 4: The Bath (Ghusl) and General Purification — A First Practical Entrance

Lesson objective: That you perform the first practical act of worship in your new life: the bath of entering Islam, and come to know a daily culture of purification.

The first practical act of worship you meet on your first day is water. It is recommended for the new Muslim to bathe with a complete bath after his Islam — covering his whole body with water, with the intention of purification and of entering his new life — following what the Sunnah indicates in the stories of the Islam of a number of the Companions, among them Thumamah ibn Uthal, as in Sahih al-Bukhari (462). And the manner of the bath, in brief definitional outline: you intend purification in your heart, and you wash your whole body with water, covering hair and skin, and if you perform wudu before it, that is more complete; and for the precise details, consult the sources referred to — for this lesson is an entrance, not a lesson in fiqh.

Then know that purification in Islam is not a single event but a daily culture: wudu before prayer, which you will master on the second rung of your ladder; a bath after major ritual impurity (janabah); purity of clothing and place; and a bodily cleanliness that the Shariah has praised to the point of making it among the acts of the fitrah: trimming the nails, removing excess hair, using the siwak, and the like, as in the hadith "Five are from the fitrah," agreed upon (al-Bukhari 5889, Muslim 257). You will quickly discover that your new religion makes your personal cleanliness an act of worship by which you draw near to Allah, not merely a social habit.

Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari (462) — the story of Thumamah and his bathing; and Sahih al-Bukhari (5889) and Sahih Muslim (257) — the hadith of the acts of the fitrah — verified via Dorar al-Sunniyah (dorar.net). Islam Question & Answer website — "Is the bath obligatory for one who becomes Muslim?" in a definitional form, with referral. IslamWeb website — the Book of Purification for beginners.

Performed practice task — “Take the step”

Take your first bath with the intention of the new beginning, and write in your notebook how you felt after it, in one sentence. Many describe this bath, years later, as their "first bodily memory" in Islam.

Companion Card (short): Detailed purification questions are referred to trustworthy fatwa sources, and the companion does not exercise his own judgement in them; the "how I felt after the bath" sentence is celebrated if the learner shares it.

Lesson 5: "Whom Do I Tell? And How?" — Family and Friends, with Wisdom

Lesson objective: That you leave with a written plan for managing the hardest social file of your first week: telling people.

The hardest questions of the first week, socially: Should I tell my family now? In what words? And what if they get angry? Here is a wise, tried plan:

First: there is no single binding religious timing for telling immediately; benefit, safety and wisdom are the measure. If your surroundings are safe and accepting, early, honest telling is easier on your heart than a long double life. And if you fear a real harm — being expelled from home, violence, or a complete cutting-off while you depend on them — then delaying the telling, while practising your religion as far as you are able, is permissible according to the people of knowledge; and ask, for your own particular case, a trustworthy fatwa source. And within the arrangements of this practical file, you may later need a shahada document proving your Islam officially, if you need it for Umrah, marriage, or an official procedure — it is a practical service that your companion arranges with you at a trusted body in your country, so do not occupy your mind with it today. Second: choose a calm moment and one person first — the closest in heart to you — and do not begin with a doctrinal debate; begin with reassurance: "I am well. I have found my way to Allah, and I love you as I am, and I will be a more dutiful son/daughter than before." Third: expect the first reaction to be shock, not a final judgement; for many families calm down when they see with their own eyes that Islam has made you more truthful, more merciful and more dutiful — not when they hear a lecture from you.

A ready-to-use conversation with an anxious mother who says, "You have left the religion of your fathers": "I have not left Allah, Mother — I have found Him. I believe in Ibrahim, Musa and 'Isa, as I always believed in them, and I have added to them my faith in Muhammad, their seal. And you will judge me by how I treat you in the coming months, not by my words today." Then hold fast to practical dutifulness; it is the most eloquent reply.

Sources: The Noble Qur'an: [Luqman: 15] on keeping company with parents in kindness even if the religion differs, [Al-'Ankabut: 8]. Islam Question & Answer website — answers on "How does the new Muslim tell his family?" and "Concealing Islam out of fear of one's family" (in definitional form, with referral of particular cases to fatwa). IslamWeb website — materials on dealing with family after Islam.

Performed practice task — “Take the step”

Write your own list: Whom will I tell first? And when? And with what opening sentence? — three lines of writing, to be reviewed with your companion before carrying it out, if you wish. And a parallel task among the level's tasks: identify the nearest trustworthy mosque or Islamic centre in your city (by searching and noting it down, without any obligation to visit it immediately).

Companion Card (short): He reviews the telling plan before it is carried out, if the learner asks; where there is a danger of real harm (expulsion / violence), a safety plan is built first and telling is not rushed; the shahada document is arranged at the first genuine practical need, not before it.

Lesson 6: The Three Mistakes of the First Week — And How to Avoid Them

Lesson objective: That you avoid, at others' expense, three mistakes that generations of new Muslims paid for: rushing, isolating, and excess.

We close your first rung with warnings from the experiences of those who came before you, so that you do not pay their price yourself:

The first mistake: total rushing — trying to change everything in a week: dress, food, friends, work and habits, all at once. Its natural result is exhaustion, then collapse, then doubt in your ability to carry the religion at all. And the treatment: the rule of this whole path — one steady rung is better than ten collapsed ones; establish prayer first (your second rung), and the rest is on its way to you, rung after rung. The second mistake: complete isolation from your old, sound surroundings, on the pretext of "the corrupt environment"; so you find yourself alone, with neither old friends nor a new Muslim community that you have not yet built — and loneliness is the most dangerous enemy of the new Muslim. And the treatment: keep your good, lawful relationships, and build your new community in parallel, on your fifth rung. The third mistake: excess in burdening yourself and others — becoming, in your first week, a harsh preacher to your family, or an executioner of yourself over every slip. Remember that your Prophet ﷺ said: "This religion is solid, so enter into it gently" — narrated by Ahmad (13052), and graded hasan by the verifiers of the Musnad — and that the most beloved deeds to Allah are the most constant of them, even if small, as in the agreed-upon hadith (al-Bukhari 6464, Muslim 783).

Sources: Musnad Ahmad (13052) — the hadith "This religion is solid, so enter into it gently"; and Sahih al-Bukhari (6464) and Sahih Muslim (783) — the hadith "The most beloved deeds to Allah are the most constant of them, even if small" — verified via Dorar al-Sunniyah (dorar.net). IslamWeb website — materials on "The mistakes of new Muslims and how they avoid them." Islam Question & Answer website — answers on gradualness and gentleness in taking the religion.

Performed practice task — “Take the step”

Complete whatever remains of the level's tasks, if anything remains: (1) Write three pressing personal questions and send them through the questions channel / your companion; (2) Read Surah al-Fatihah in full, with the approved translation of its meanings in your language — for it is your coming prayer, and the beginning of your fourth rung, later.

Companion Card (short): At the level-closing meeting: the list of the three questions, the telling plan and the complete practice log are reviewed, the learner is celebrated with the rung celebration (see the celebration moment below), and the second rung is opened together.

Completing the First Rung: The Completion Standard and the Celebration Moment

Completion standard: This level is complete when the average of its lesson quizzes reaches at least 80% — attempts are taken in sequence, each new attempt unlocked only after a guided review task is completed, and the best attempt counts — together with completion of all the level's practice tasks. No certificate is issued for completing this level; instead, its rung on the ladder lights up, and the next rung opens. (Specification 4.1, as amended by the approved indication R2-D17 — IOU mechanics without certificates: the average of the six lesson quizzes is at least 80%; attempts are sequential in the documented pattern: the next attempt is opened only after completing a guided review task — "Go back to paragraph such-and-such of lesson such-and-such" — instead of any silent time interval, and the highest counts; plus completion of all six performed practice tasks in your log. Then your ladder lights up its first rung in full — and no certificate of any kind is issued on completion; the lit rung is the mark.) Achievement mark (no completion certificate — the owner's decision): A first lit rung on "the ladder that leads to the Light" in your account + the message "You have reached the first rung" — and your companion reviews your log and congratulates you personally at the level-closing meeting, then the second rung opens. Exit checklist for this rung (declared exit criteria, in the Kawthar style — R2-D16; shown to the learner before the rung message):

Text of the celebration moment (shown to the learner on completion): "You have completed your first week as a Muslim — the hardest week, and the most beautiful. You pronounced, you understood, you bathed for your new beginning, you wrote your questions without fear, and you set your plan for your family with wisdom. Your first rung is lit now on your ladder, and your companion is proud of you. The second rung awaits you: the water of wudu and your first complete prayer — take your time, for the ladder does not run away, and your lit rungs do not go out."
