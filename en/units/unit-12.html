Unit Twelve — How Do I Become Muslim? Your First Step and What Comes After It

WayToNoor — «طريق النور» — "Your way to the Light of Islam — with a companion for your first steps."

1. Opening Hook

Twelve units, and the questions of a whole lifetime, all lead to a single short sentence. No ceremonies, no fees, no intermediaries, and no examination before it. In this short unit: your final questions and their answers, the sentence word by word as it is pronounced, and the story of a man who stood before you at the same door and stipulated a single condition — so hear what was said to him.

2. Executive Summary

Entering Islam is a conscious pronouncement of the two testimonies, with understanding, consent and sincerity — by it you are a Muslim, complete in Islam, between you and Allah, and you need no intermediary, no permission and no fees. Whatever sins came before it, Islam wipes them out completely: "Islam wipes out what came before it", and people's rights are returned as far as one is able. And at the moment of pronunciation, the "First 7 Days" pack and a named companion reach you immediately, and you begin the first rung on "the ladder to the Light" toward a new, gradual life — not a chaotic upheaval in a single day. And if one "but…" remains in your chest, it has a whole series by name at the end of the unit.

3. Before You Begin: Prerequisites and Your Place in the Path

This unit is the conclusion of the whole of Part One; it was written for whoever read the path or most of it and found acceptance. It does not assume you have "mastered" anything — for learning begins after it, not before it, and the "First 30 Days" path (the five levels of the ladder that leads to the Light) is your next ready home. And whoever reached it hesitant, not yet decided, let him read it as a calm reading of information; for the door of questions and companionship for seekers does not stop at non-decision.

4. Unit Objectives

By the end of this unit you will be able to: - Answer the common final questions before the step (complete learning? changing name and life? past sins? intermediaries and fees?). - Pronounce the two testimonies with precise understanding of the meaning of every word in them, and know the two pronunciation options (with a human being / alone) and what follows from each of them immediately. - Explain the promise "Islam wipes out what came before it" and its limits regarding the rights of servants. - Describe the plan of your first days and the "First 7 Days" pack and the place of the practical Shahada Document in it, and name your barrier if one remains in the "I want to become Muslim, but…" series.

5. Main Text

5.1 First: Your Final Questions Are Legitimate, So Hear Their Answers Before Any Step

We know what is going around in your head now, because thousands before you said it aloud: "Must I learn everything first?" No; Islam is entered by the two testimonies, then learning begins rung by rung, as you saw in the unit on the pillars, and the Prophet ﷺ said to a man who confined himself to the five obligations: "He will succeed, if he is truthful." "Must I change my name, my clothes and my whole life tomorrow?" No; your name remains your name unless it carries an explicitly forbidden meaning, and your life changes by reordering priorities, not by a chaotic upheaval in a single day. "What about all my past sins?" They have a whole section shortly, and its summary is more beautiful than you expect. "Do I need an intermediary, ceremonies, fees, or anyone's permission?" None of that at all: no priest, no church, no fees, and not even the presence of an imam as a condition for the validity of your Islam between you and Allah.

Entering Islam is simpler than you imagine, and deeper than you imagine, at one and the same time: two phrases you pronounce with understanding, consent and sincerity, and then you are a Muslim, complete in Islam, a brother to two billion human beings, with what is theirs for you and what is upon them upon you.

Sources: - Sahih al-Bukhari (46) and Sahih Muslim (11) — the hadith of Talhah ibn 'Ubaydullah, "He will succeed, if he is truthful" — verification via Dorar Al-Saniyyah (dorar.net). - Islam Q&A (islamqa.info) — the answer to "How does a person enter Islam?" and "What is required of a new Muslim first?". - Islamweb (islamweb.net) — materials on non-Muslims becoming Muslim.

5.2 The Two Testimonies, Word by Word: What Exactly Do You Say, and What Does It Mean?

You say: "Ashhadu an la ilaha illallah, wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah" — "I bear witness that there is no deity but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." And before you pronounce it, hear it first as it is pronounced: in this unit's audio there is a slow human clip pronouncing it syllable by syllable — Ash-ha-du… an la ila-ha… il-lal-lah — for you to repeat after it at ease until it settles in your ear and on your tongue. If you cannot yet pronounce it in Arabic, pronounce its translation in your language with the intention of entering Islam, and learn its Arabic pronunciation in your following days without embarrassment; for what counts is the understood, intended meaning, not the soundness of the articulation points from the first time.

"Ashhadu" (I bear witness): I affirm in my heart and declare with my tongue, the affirmation of one who has seen and known, not blind imitation — and it is a testimony with which Allah Himself begins, and His angels and those of knowledge: "Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him, and [so do] the angels and those of knowledge - [that He is] maintaining [creation] in justice" [Aal 'Imran: 18]. "An la ilaha illallah" (that there is no deity but Allah): there is no one worshipped in truth in existence except Allah, the One, the Unique, whom you knew in Unit Two; so I single Him out with my worship, my supplication, my fear and my hope, and I renounce every worshipped thing besides Him. "Wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah" (and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah): I affirm that Muhammad ﷺ is the servant of Allah and His final Messenger, whose biography you knew in Unit Five; so I obey him in what he commanded, believe him in what he told, and worship Allah according to his guidance.

With this conscious pronouncement, you have become Muslim. It is recommended that two Muslims witness with you if that is possible — for announcement, joy and social documentation, not for the validity of Islam itself — and that you perform a complete bath (ghusl) after it, as the Sunnah indicated in the story of Thumamah ibn Uthal's Islam and others, then you begin the journey of learning. And at this very moment you have two explicit options, no third, in the design of this page: "I want to pronounce it now with a human being" — and a named companion will reach you, from whom you hear it and with whom you pronounce it directly; or "I want to pronounce it alone and begin my journey" — and you pronounce it now, where you are, and there is opened to you, at the same moment, the "First 7 Days" pack and your first rung on the ladder that leads to the Light. Neither option is more correct than the other; the most correct is what your heart is at ease with.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Muhammad: 19], [Aal 'Imran: 18]. - Sahih al-Bukhari (462) — the story of Thumamah ibn Uthal's Islam and his bath, in the Book of Prayer — verification via Dorar Al-Saniyyah (dorar.net). - Islam Q&A — "How does a person enter Islam?"; and Islamweb — "The manner of entering Islam for the new person".

5.3 A Story That Opens the Door Wide for You: "Will You Extend Your Hand?"

'Amr ibn al-'As, may Allah be pleased with him, came to the Prophet ﷺ to pledge allegiance to him upon Islam, and before that he had been among the fiercest opponents of the call. He said: He ﷺ extended his hand for me to pledge to him, and I withdrew my hand. He said: "What is the matter with you, O 'Amr?" I said: I wanted to stipulate a condition. He said: "Stipulate what?" I said: That I be forgiven. Then the Prophet ﷺ said a word that ought to be written in gold at the door of every new entrant: "Did you not know that Islam wipes out what came before it, and that migration wipes out what came before it, and that Hajj wipes out what came before it?" — narrated by Muslim (121). "Yajubbu" (wipes out): it cuts and erases; a complete, new blank page, with no archive and no previous debts with Allah.

And Allah, exalted is He, said, addressing those who have transgressed against themselves with all sins: "Say, 'O My servants who have transgressed against themselves [by sinning], do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful'" [Az-Zumar: 53]. And this door of return is open in the form of an absolute promise to everyone who wrongs himself then seeks forgiveness: "And whoever does a wrong or wrongs himself but then seeks forgiveness of Allah will find Allah Forgiving and Merciful" [An-Nisa: 110]. As for the material rights of servants — money you took, or a right a person has against you — their wisdom is more beautiful: Islam wipes out what is between you and Allah, and teaches you to return what you can of people's rights or seek their pardon; so your new door is clean from both sides as far as you are able, and Allah takes charge of what you were unable to do, by the sincerity of your intention.

If there is now in your chest a voice saying: "I, specifically, do not deserve it; my past is too heavy to be forgiven" — then this story and this verse were written for you, almost by name. There is no past greater than Allah's mercy for whoever is sincere in returning to Him.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Az-Zumar: 53], [An-Nisa: 110]. - Sahih Muslim (121) — the hadith of 'Amr ibn al-'As, "Islam wipes out what came before it" — verification via Dorar Al-Saniyyah (dorar.net). - Islam Q&A — answers to "Does Allah forgive a new Muslim what came before?" with religious review.

5.4 "I Want to Become Muslim, But…": Barriers With Names, and Answers With Titles

If there is one "but" in your chest that has not yet dissolved, you are in good company — most of those who entered before you stopped at one of these very ones. For this reason we designed a short series called "I want to become Muslim, but…", for each barrier a short, independent piece linked to a specific lesson from the first level on the ladder that leads to the Light: But my family — fear of the reaction of family and surroundings (linked to the lesson on managing telling others with wisdom and gentleness); But the change is big — fear of overturning life all at once (linked to the lesson on gradualness: one rung a week); But I am not perfect — fear of a repeated sin or a habit not yet abandoned (linked to the lesson "Islam wipes out what came before it" and the open door of repentance); But what will people say? — social fear (linked to the lesson on building a new surroundings gradually). Choose your barrier by its name and read its piece; and if you do not find your barrier's name in the list, write it to us — for the list grows with the questions of real people like you.

Sources: - Islam Q&A and Islamweb — answers to new Muslims' fears (family, gradualness, repeated sins) — selected for each piece in the series, with referral. - Sahih Muslim (121) — the frame of "Islam wipes out what came before it" for the "I am not perfect" piece.

5.5 The First 72 Hours After the Shahada: A Small Plan That Does Not Frighten

So that the joy of the decision does not turn into the bewilderment of "What do I do now?", here are the steps of your first three days, all of them from the level "The Shahada and the First Week" — your first rung on the ladder that leads to the Light — and they come to you complete in the "First 7 Days" pack that is opened to you at the moment of pronunciation:

Day one: Perform the bath (ghusl), and pray two rak'ahs of thanks if you are able to learn them, and tell whoever you trust among your family or friends the news with wisdom and gentleness — or postpone telling a little if you fear definite harm, for the Sharia does not charge you with what you cannot bear — and contact your named companion, who will receive the pack with you step by step. Day two: Begin learning wudu and Al-Fatihah with a translation of its meanings, and listen to a slow recitation of it in the voice of a real reciter until it settles in your ear. Day three: Visit the nearest trusted mosque or Islamic centre in your city on a short visit of acquaintance, or contact it by phone if visiting is not possible; for you need real faces who know you by your name, not pages only. And within the practical arrangements of these days you may need a Shahada Document proving your Islam officially if you want Umrah, marriage, or an official procedure that requests it — it is a practical service your companion arranges with you at the trusted body in your country; so do not occupy your mind with it today — it is enough to know it exists and is available when you need it.

And there also awaits you a welcome befitting the greatest decision of your life: a celebration of your entering Islam that gathers you — in an online meeting — with the founder and with scholars and teachers who congratulate you and answer your first questions, and a personal celebration video made for you, personally, may reach you; it is a celebration of the joy of your Islam, not an official honours ceremony, and it is yours alone, to keep for yourself, and nothing of it is ever published except with your explicit permission. And what comes after it is drawn for you, rung by rung, on the ladder that leads to the Light: a prayer that gradually reaches the five, calm preparation for your first Ramadan, and a beginning in Qur'an and Arabic with a live teacher at Thiqa Academy when you reach its rung, and a community and character that firm your feet until reaching the Light. And every piece you complete in your journey lights up a new rung on your ladder and opens the one after it, until you hear the phrase this whole project was made for you to hear one day: "You have reached the Light." You are not alone on any rung of it; this is the phrase upon which this whole site was built.

Sources: - Islam Q&A and Islamweb — guides to "The first thing a new Muslim does" (in the site's approved gradual formulation). - Sahih Muslim (121) — the frame of repentance and forgiveness for the new Muslim.

5.6 A Final Message Before You Close This Page

My seeker friend: you began this path, twelve units ago, with a question in the quiet of the night: Where did I come from, why am I here, and where am I going? And you have walked with us through the whole answer: Allah, the One, the Merciful; the noble purpose; the prophets in a single chain; Muhammad, the final one, the human being; the preserved Qur'an; the pillars, ethics and family; and the hardest questions without evasion. We asked nothing of you in the first unit, and we put no pressure on you in the last unit; for a decision that is not born free does not live long.

But we say to you with the honesty of friends: if you have found in this path an answer with which your mind and your heart are both at ease, do not postpone a step you know is truth for the sake of fears that are resolved on the way; for everyone who walked before you discovered that most of his fears were illusions from afar, and that the door, from inside, is much wider than it appeared from outside. And if you are still hesitant, the door of questions is open, and our companionship of you as a seeker does not stop at your non-decision; come back whenever you wish, ask whatever you wish, and compare with whatever you wish.

"And say, 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge'" [Taha: 114]. We will see you — God willing — on the first rung of the ladder that leads to the Light, or on the questions page, whichever you choose. Welcome, in either case.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Taha: 114], [Az-Zumar: 53]. - Sahih Muslim (121) — the hadith of 'Amr ibn al-'As, may Allah be pleased with him. - Islamweb — the da'wah and introduction-to-Islam section, materials on accompanying new Muslims.

7. Your Question

What is stopping you — with complete honesty between you and yourself? Name it in a single sentence: fear of family? Of change? Of a sin you have not been able to leave? Of people's talk? Write its sentence as it is; if you find its name in the "I want to become Muslim, but…" series (section 5.4), begin with its piece, and if you do not find it, send it to us so a piece is written for it — for you are not the first to stand at this door, and you will not be the last, and no one here will judge you by the name of your barrier.

9. Your One Next Step

One step, and it is the step of a lifetime: Pronounce it — with a human being or alone, as in section 5.2 — and there will be opened to you immediately the "First 7 Days" pack and your first rung on the ladder that leads to the Light, and the first level in the "First 30 Days" path: "The Shahada and the First Week". 10. Companion Card and Published Times

  • Ready discussion points: (1) Readiness for the moment of pronunciation: if the learner chooses the first button, the named companion is present in a calm voice, hears the Shahada from him first, slowly, then pronounces it with him — with no audience and no filming except with explicit, revocable permission. (2) Immediately after pronunciation: a short, warm congratulation, then opening the Seven Days pack together and explaining the step of day one only (the whole plan is not delivered all at once). (3) The Shahada Document: mentioned in a single practical line within the arrangements of the first days if required (Umrah/marriage/an official procedure) and arranged at a trusted body — and never presented as a certificate of achievement.
  • Suggested follow-up questions: How did you feel an hour after pronunciation, and after a day? Did you tell anyone? And how did they receive it? What was the hardest moment in the first 72 hours?
  • Warning signs and escalation: The Shahada celebration (the welcome meeting / the personal celebration video) is private to the new Muslim alone — no publication or sharing of any part of it except with his explicit, documented permission, and its privacy is sacred; acute family/social pressure after announcement ← a calm safety plan with the learner (postponing telling, alternative community support) and he is not left alone in the confrontation; repeated hesitation at the threshold for months ← his rhythm is respected completely and he is followed up as a seeker with no pressure at all (a free decision is the condition of a long life); any request to film/publish the story of his Islam ← explicit, documented, revocable permission before any use, and refusal is respected without discussion; precise jurisprudential questions after entry ← trusted fatwa sources and religious review, not the companion's independent reasoning.
  • The piece's place in the weekly work plan: This unit is the beginning of the actual companionship contract: a fixed weekly meeting for the first 12 weeks (extendable by agreement), the Level 1 practices register reviewed at every meeting, and celebration of every new rung on the ladder that leads to the Light at the moment it happens, not at a later date.
  • Published times: Reading the text about 7 minutes · Watching the video 3–5 minutes · Listening to the slow pronunciation of the Shahada about 2 minutes (production estimates, as labelled in the Arabic edition).
