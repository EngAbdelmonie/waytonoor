Unit Six — The Qur'an: The Preserved Word of Allah

WayToNoor — «طريق النور» — "Your way to the Light of Islam — with a companion for your first steps."

Qur'an quotations follow the Saheeh International translation. Hadith quotations follow recognised published English translations, with the same source references (takhrij) as the Arabic edition.

1. Opening Hook

At this very moment in which you are reading these lines, somewhere on this earth, a child of seven is reciting from his memory, a grandmother of ninety is reciting the same text, and a young memoriser on another continent is reciting it too — in one letter that does not differ between them. A book fourteen centuries old, memorised by millions of human beings by heart, and of which there are no "contested copies" as with many ancient texts. How did it remain? This unit opens the preservation file for you, from its beginning.

2. Executive Summary

The Qur'an among the Muslims is the word of Allah revealed to Muhammad ﷺ: one Book of 114 surahs, revealed over 23 years, written from its very first moment by the scribes of the revelation, memorised in the breasts of hundreds of the Companions, then compiled into one mushaf within the lifetime of the first generation itself. And the Muslims' proofs of the soundness of its text are historical and open to examination: an unbroken mass transmission in the breasts until today, early manuscripts whose text matches the text in circulation, and the unity of the text in the whole world. Arabic is its preserved origin, against which translations are measured; as for understanding its meaning, it is available in more than one hundred approved translations of its meanings. And the best entrance to it is to read it yourself — and this unit suggests to you where to begin.

3. Before You Begin: Prerequisites and Your Place in the Path

This is Unit 6 of 12. It presupposes Unit Five, which introduced you to the one who brought the Qur'an and to the proofs of his truthfulness; this unit looks at the message itself: what is it? And how did it reach us? It stands on its own for whoever came to it from a direct search for "the Qur'an," and its place in the path prepares for Unit Seven: how does this Book turn into a daily life?

4. Unit Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to: - Distinguish precisely between the Qur'an, the Prophetic hadith, and the books of biography and exegesis. - Narrate the story of the Qur'an's revelation, writing and compilation in its major chronological stages. - Present the Muslims' three proofs of the text's preservation: mass transmission, the early manuscripts, and the worldwide unity of the text. - Explain why the Arabic origin remained the reference, what the place of the translations of the meanings is, and where to begin your own reading.

5. Main Text

5.1 What Is the Qur'an? One Book, Not a Scattered Library

The Qur'an among the Muslims is the word of Allah revealed to Muhammad ﷺ by revelation, worshipped through its recitation, preserved in the breasts and the lines until today. The Qur'an itself described the manner of its revelation in this description: ﴿The Trustworthy Spirit [i.e., Gabriel] has brought it down upon your heart, [O Muhammad] — that you may be of the warners — in a clear Arabic language﴾ [Ash-Shu'ara: 193–195] — Gabriel, peace be upon him (the Trustworthy Spirit), brought it down upon the Prophet's heart ﷺ, in clear Arabic. And it is one integrated Book: 114 surahs, whose verses were revealed separately over twenty-three years according to events, questions and circumstances, then were gathered between the two covers of one mushaf within the lifetime of the first generation itself.

It is important that you distinguish between three things that many people confuse: the Qur'an, which is the revelation itself, which the Muslims believe is the word of Allah in letter and meaning; the Prophetic hadith, which is the words of the Prophet ﷺ and his guidance, transmitted with a unique scholarly care but which is not Qur'an; and the books of biography, jurisprudence and exegesis, which are human efforts in the history of the message and its understanding. So if you read a verse on this site, it is from the first alone; if you read a hadith, you will find it referenced with its source and its grade; and if you read an explanation, it is the scholars' understanding, not new revelation.

The Qur'an speaks about Allah, man, purpose, the prophets, destiny, morality and legislation, and addresses its reader directly in the form of call, command and question, until its reader feels — as many new readers testify — that he is being addressed personally, not that he is reading a cold historical document.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Baqarah: 2], [Ash-Shu'ara: 192–195], [Yusuf: 2]. - Manna' Al-Qattan, Mabahith fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an (Studies in the Sciences of the Qur'an) — the chapters defining the Qur'an and revelation, and the difference between the Qur'an and the hadith. - IslamWeb website (islamweb.net) — the Qur'anic Sciences section.

5.2 How Was It Revealed, Written and Compiled? A Story of Early Documentation

The first revelation came down in the cave of Hira around the year 610 CE, and its descent continued in Makkah then Madinah until shortly before the Prophet's death ﷺ in the year 632 CE. And from the very first moment, the transmission of the Qur'an did not rely on memory alone: the Prophet ﷺ took scribes of the revelation who would write what came down immediately upon its descent, on whatever writing materials were available, and he ﷺ would review with Gabriel, peace be upon him, what had come down every year, and in the final year he reviewed it twice. And the Qur'an was memorised in the breasts of hundreds of the Companions, completely, in his lifetime ﷺ and under his supervision. Allah had undertaken, in the Qur'an itself, its collection and its clarification from the time of the revelation: ﴿Indeed, upon Us is its collection [in your heart] and [to make possible] its recitation. So when We have recited it [through Gabriel], then follow its recitation. Then upon Us is its clarification [to you]﴾ [Al-Qiyamah: 17–19] — so the collection is a divine promise fulfilled by documented human means that history relates.

Shortly after his death ﷺ, in the caliphate of Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, the written sheets were gathered from their scattered sources into one collection kept with the caliph, at the initiative of Umar ibn Al-Khattab and carried out by Zayd ibn Thabit — one of the scribes of the revelation themselves — who required, for accepting every verse, two witnesses to its having been written in the Prophet's presence ﷺ. Then, in the caliphate of Uthman ibn Affan, may Allah be pleased with him, and with the expansion of the state and the entry of new nations into Islam, those sheets were copied into unified official mushafs sent to the major cities, and one of them remained famous by the name "the Imam Mushaf" (Al-Mushaf Al-Imam). Thus the documentation of the text in writing was completed within about two decades of the Prophet's death ﷺ, in the lifetime of thousands who had heard it from him directly — a very early documentation period by the standards of the history of all ancient texts.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Qiyamah: 17–19], [Al-Hijr: 9]. - Sahih al-Bukhari (4986) — the hadith of the compilation of the Qur'an in the era of Abu Bakr and Uthman, may Allah be pleased with them both, via Dorar Al-Sunnah for verification. - Manna' Al-Qattan, Mabahith fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an (Studies in the Sciences of the Qur'an) — the chapters on the compilation of the Qur'an and its writing down. - The King Fahd Complex for the Printing of the Holy Mushaf — materials on the history of the writing and compilation of the mushaf.

5.3 What Are the Muslims' Proofs That the Text Did Not Change?

The Muslims say that Allah undertook the preservation of this Book Himself: ﴿Indeed, it is We who sent down the message [i.e., the Qur'an], and indeed, We will be its guardian﴾ [Al-Hijr: 9]. And He described it as a Book that falsehood cannot approach from any side: ﴿And indeed, it is a mighty Book. Falsehood cannot approach it from before it or from behind it; [it is] a revelation from a [Lord who is] Wise and Praiseworthy﴾ [Fussilat: 41–42]. And they present for its preservation historical proofs that any researcher can examine:

First: the continuous mass transmission in the breasts. Millions of memorisers have transmitted the Qur'an by heart, generation after generation, with a connected chain to the Prophet ﷺ until today, such that if a memoriser erred in one letter, the children of the circles would correct him before their elders did. Second: the early manuscripts. Research at the British University of Birmingham in the year 2015 CE revealed that folios of a Qur'anic manuscript preserved there date, by radiocarbon — with high probability — to between the years 568 and 645 CE, that is, to the era of the Prophet ﷺ or the first decades immediately after him, and that their text matches the text in circulation today. And the libraries of the world, the manuscripts of Sanaa and others preserve early copies confirming the same stability. Third: the worldwide unity of the text. The Muslim in Jakarta, the Muslim in Dakar and the Muslim in London read today from one mushaf in the same letters, and correct one another in a single prayer with no difference at all in the text — a scene for which there is hardly any parallel among the texts of the ancient world.

And with this preservation, the Qur'an has challenged, since its revelation, anyone to bring its like, or the like of one of its surahs: ﴿And if you are in doubt about what We have sent down [i.e., the Qur'an] upon Our Servant [i.e., Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ)], then produce a surah the like thereof and call upon your witnesses [i.e., supporters] other than Allah, if you should be truthful﴾ [Al-Baqarah: 23]. And this challenge has remained standing for fourteen centuries, and the Book itself remained as it is, recited at every moment of the night and the day somewhere on this earth.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Hijr: 9], [Al-Baqarah: 23], [Fussilat: 41–42]. - The University of Birmingham — its official research statement in the year 2015 on the radiocarbon dating of the folios of the Birmingham Qur'anic manuscript. - Manna' Al-Qattan, Mabahith fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an (Studies in the Sciences of the Qur'an) — the chapter on the preservation of the Qur'an and its mass transmission. - IslamWeb website — the Qur'anic Sciences section, entries on "The Proofs of the Preservation of the Qur'an."

5.4 Why Arabic Specifically? And What Is the Place of the Translations?

Many ask: why do the Muslims insist on reading the Qur'an in Arabic, even those of them who do not speak it? The answer is from the heart of the very idea of preservation: the Qur'an was revealed in the language of the Messenger's first people — ﴿Indeed, We have sent it down as an Arabic Qur'an that you might understand﴾ [Yusuf: 2] — so its Arabic origin remained the preserved reference against which every translation is measured, and the whims of translators across the centuries cannot slip into it as they slip into translated texts when their origin is lost. Arabic here is not racial sanctity, but a textual safety valve: two billion Muslims, in hundreds of languages, all return to one origin that does not multiply.

As for understanding the meaning, it is not conditional on your being an Arab; the meanings of the Qur'an have been translated into more than one hundred languages, and these translations are counted among the Muslims as "translations of the meanings of the Qur'an," not a substitute Qur'an, because they are a human effort in conveying the meaning that admits error and revision. Among the most reliable English translations in circulation and approved in Islamic institutions: the translation of "Saheeh International," the historical translation of Abdullah Yusuf Ali, the translation of Muhammad Abdel Haleem published by Oxford University, and the official translations of the King Fahd Complex in the languages of the world. So choose from them what you find in your language, and begin reading; the meaning will reach you, and the origin is preserved for whoever wants to go deeper one day.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Yusuf: 2], [Fussilat: 44], [Ibrahim: 4]. - The King Fahd Complex for the Printing of the Holy Mushaf — the index of the approved translations of the meanings of the Qur'an in the languages of the world. - Manna' Al-Qattan, Mabahith fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an (Studies in the Sciences of the Qur'an) — the chapter on the translation of the Qur'an and its scholarly rulings. - IslamWeb website — entries on "Reliable Translations of the Meanings of the Qur'an."

5.5 The Readings and the Uthmanic Script: Preserved Diversity, Not Contested Difference

A precise question asked by one who has read a little about the history of texts: "I have heard that the Qur'an has multiple readings; is that not a difference in the text?" The answer, in honest brief: the readings among the Muslims are mass-transmitted modes of pronouncing the letters and words, transmitted by the same chain by which the text itself was transmitted, and they all return to what the revelation brought down and the Prophet ﷺ approved for his companions from the Arab tribes of differing dialects. They are a diversity in the vocal performance of some words within one textual structure, fixed in letters in the Imam Mushaf — like the difference in pronouncing a word between two ancient Arabic dialects — not competing texts, and not verses added and removed. That is why you find a memoriser reading in the narration of Hafs in the East and another reading in the narration of Warsh in the Arab Maghreb; and if you opened their two mushafs, you would find the same verses, the same letters and the same surahs; the difference is in how some words are pronounced, not in the body of the Book. And the Uthmanic script — the way words were written in the first mushafs, without dots or vowel marks in its origin — was the written regulator that accommodated all these modes and disciplined them, so the readings remained a science governed by rules and chain, not an open door in which every reader reads as he wishes.

So if one day you hear someone say "there exist different versions of the Qur'an," ask him calmly: show me two different surahs between two mushafs printed on two different continents. He will not find any; because the unity of the body is a fact verifiable in any library in the world within minutes, and it is itself among the strongest of what the Muslims present in the entire preservation file.

Sources: - Manna' Al-Qattan, Mabahith fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an (Studies in the Sciences of the Qur'an) — the chapters on the readings and the Uthmanic script. - IslamWeb website (islamweb.net) — the Qur'anic Sciences section, entries on "The Mass-Transmitted Readings and the Difference Between Them and Differences of Texts."

5.6 The Early Manuscripts: What Do the Libraries Actually Say?

We mentioned in section 5.3 the Birmingham manuscript as one witness; the complete picture is wider and stronger than one witness. The great libraries of the world — from Sanaa to Istanbul to Paris, Cairo and St. Petersburg — preserve thousands of Qur'anic manuscript pages from the first Hijri centuries, written by calligraphers in far-apart cities who did not know one another; and when they are compared with the text of the mushaf printed today, they come out matching it in the body, their difference being in marginal decorations and scribal additions, not in the verses. And the famous Sanaa manuscripts — discovered in the Great Mosque of Sanaa and studied by German and French missions over decades — showed an old lower text and an upper text matching the mushaf in circulation, and their studies revolved around the history of writing and the order of the surahs in the first personal mushafs, not around a different "other Qur'an" in content; there is not, in any library in the world, an ancient Qur'anic manuscript relating a different story, a different prophet or a different law.

And this is a methodological point that deserves your pause as a fair researcher: the file of the Qur'an's preservation is not a file of "religious trust" that you are asked to believe by faith alone; it is a historical file open in the libraries, the museums and the academic journals, examined by Muslims and non-Muslims with the same tools — carbon dating, analysis of ink and parchment, and comparison of texts — and the more the tools of examination advance, the more the text increases in stability in its place. Compare that with the fates of many ancient texts whose followers possess, from their first centuries, nothing but copies centuries later than their origins, and you will realise why the Muslims present the preservation file with calm confidence, not defensive emotion.

Sources: - The University of Birmingham — the official research statement (2015) on the Birmingham Qur'anic manuscript. - Manna' Al-Qattan, Mabahith fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an (Studies in the Sciences of the Qur'an) — the chapter on the compilation of the Qur'an and the first mushafs. - IslamWeb website — the Qur'anic Sciences section, entries on "The Early Qur'anic Manuscripts and Their Indications."

5.7 Where Do You Begin Reading It Yourself? A Practical Invitation

We close this unit with a practical step instead of theoretical talk, for the Qur'an is a Book that prefers to present itself by itself. If you want to begin today, here is a suggested reading map for the new seeker:

Begin with Surat Al-Fatihah, the Mother of the Book, which the Muslim reads in every rak'ah; then with Surat Maryam, which relates the story of Mary and Jesus, peace be upon them, in captivating emotional language; then with Surat Yusuf, of which its own Book said: ﴿We relate to you, [O Muhammad], the best of stories﴾ [Yusuf: 3] — a complete story from its beginning to its end in one surah. Then move to Surahs Al-Kahf and Ya-Sin, and read slowly and reflectively, following the verse that describes the manner of its recitation: ﴿and recite the Qur'an with measured recitation﴾ [Al-Muzzammil: 4] — a slow, reflective measured recitation with no haste in it. And write down the verses and questions that stop you. And do not read the whole Qur'an in one go with the eye of the critic searching only for error, nor with the eye of the one who surrenders without a question, but with a third, fairer eye: the eye of the sincere seeker who wants to know what this Book actually says, before judging it by what others said about it.

And you will quickly notice that the Qur'an is not read as novels are read; it is a discourse of guidance moving between belief, story, parable, legislation, promise and warning, because it is a Book of a complete life, not a message on a single subject. And with every reading you will find new layers — and this itself is part of what the Muslims say about its inimitability: one Book read by the Bedouin, the university professor, the child and the philosopher, and each of them finds in it his own address.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Fatihah: 1–7], [Yusuf: 3], [Al-Muzzammil: 4]. - IslamWeb website — guides to reading the Qur'an for beginners, and the suggested order of surahs for seekers. - Islam Question & Answer website — answers on "With What Does a Non-Muslim Begin Reading the Qur'an?"

5.8 The Qur'an in the Muslim's Life Today: A Book Lived, Not Merely Shelved

Close the unit with a look at what this preserved Book does in the lives of its people today, so you see the fruit of preservation with your own eyes. The Qur'an is the most recited text on the face of the earth at every moment; the five prayers are opened with it in every mosque and wherever a Muslim prays; the whole month of Ramadan is concluded with it in the Tarawih prayers in most of the world's mosques, so millions of Muslims hear it by heart from their imams in a single month; councils, celebrations and new homes are opened with it; and it is recited at birth into the newborn's ear and at dying into the hearing of the dying. And its children memorise it young in the memorisation circles spread from Indonesia to Mauritania to the mosques of Europe and America, until "Al-Hafiz" (the Memoriser) became a title every Muslim neighbourhood knows in one person or more of its neighbours. This dense daily presence is itself part of the mechanism of preservation across the centuries: the text that lives in the breasts, the mosques, the homes and the occasions does not need an archive to protect it from loss; the whole nation is its living, moving archive.

And perhaps the truest thing with which we close this file: the Muslims do not deal with their Qur'an as merely a "well-preserved historical document" — for that is the rank of the academics — but as a living discourse addressing every reader in his time: you find the man reading a verse in his trial and feeling that it came down for him today; you find the mother reading the verses of healing over her sick child; and you find the perplexed young man opening the mushaf to a verse and finding in it the answer to his question, which he had asked no one. This living interaction between a fixed text and ever-renewed readers is what made the Qur'an — after fourteen centuries of literal preservation — a Book that does not age. So if you want to sum up the file of this whole unit in one sentence to carry with you: the Qur'an is the only book in the history of humanity whose textual soundness you can verify yourself by three independent routes — comparing its first manuscripts in the museums with today's mushaf; hearing its memorisers on different continents reciting one text; and opening mushafs printed in the furthest East and the furthest West and finding the same letters — and each of these routes alone suffices for the reassurance of a fair mind, so how about them combined? That is why the Muslims do not present their faith in the preservation of their Book as merely a "doctrine to be surrendered to," but as a historical case open to examination, which they invite you to examine before they ask you to believe it. And that is why this unit does not end for you with a piece of information, but begins for you with a habit: from today, when you hear a verse recited somewhere or see an open mushaf on a table, you will know that behind these pages stand fourteen centuries of scribes, memorisers, manuscripts and libraries in a single rank, to say to you: this text reached you as it was revealed, letter by letter. And your only responsibility now is not to "believe us"; it is to open the Book itself and read — for it was not preserved with all this preservation so that you would admire its preservation from afar, but so that it would reach you, and so that you would read it. And in the next unit you will move from the Book to what the Book builds in a human being's life every day: the five pillars of Islam — to discover that the preserved text whose file you have now completed was not preserved to remain in the mushafs, but to turn into a day that is lived, a conduct that is seen, and a complete life built upon it.

Sources: - IslamWeb website (islamweb.net) — the Qur'anic Sciences section, entries on "The Presence of the Qur'an in the Lives of the Muslims." - The King Fahd Complex for the Printing of the Holy Mushaf — materials on the spread of the mushaf and its printings in the world.

6. Key Terms

Term Pronunciation Brief meaning
Revelation Al-Waḥy What Allah revealed to His prophet of His word and His guidance, through Gabriel, peace be upon him
Mass transmission At-Tawātur Transmission by a multitude whose collusion upon a lie is impossible, from a multitude like them, generation after generation
Scribes of the revelation Kuttāb Al-Waḥy The Companions who used to write the Qur'an immediately upon its revelation, by the Prophet's command ﷺ
The Imam Mushaf Al-Muṣḥaf Al-Imām The unified official mushaf copied in the era of Uthman, may Allah be pleased with him, and sent to the cities

7. Your Question

Have you ever tried to listen to the Qur'an with complete attention — not as background music, but as a discourse directed at you, personally — even for five minutes? What do you expect you would feel? Guided listening (this unit's task): Listen to a slow, clear recitation, in the voice of a real human reciter, of one short surah (Al-Fatihah or Al-Ikhlas, for example), then write one line: what was the first impression it left in you? — This line is the seed of the "7-Day Listening Challenge" that will reach you in your next step, if you wish.
