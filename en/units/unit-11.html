Unit Eleven — Common Questions and Major Objections

WayToNoor — «طريق النور» — "Your way to the Light of Islam — with a companion for your first steps."

1. Opening Hook

Five questions every seeker wishes he could ask and does not dare: violence, evil, science, freedom of belief, and the multiplicity of religions. In this unit you will not hear impressionistic answers; you will hear the objection first, in the strongest form its proponents state it — with the verses they cite, complete — then the answer from the complete text and its context. If you come out with a question we have not answered, you have at the end of the unit an open channel called: "Present Your Objection".

2. Executive Summary

The strongest objections of the age against Islam are five, and each of them has an answer from inside the texts themselves: the verses of fighting were revealed in a besieged, defensive context and with early ethical controls, and the text itself commands righteousness toward the peaceful and justice toward them, and granting protection to whoever seeks it until he reaches his place of safety; and the multiplicity of religions, for Muslims, is a willed trial of choice that Allah willed, not an incapacity, with a precise distinction between the doctrinal position and the human duty; and the problem of evil is answered by three rules: human evil for which the free human being is responsible, pain as a trial in a temporary abode whose reward is in an abiding abode, and ignorance of wisdom that does not negate it; and science, for Muslims, is worship through reflection, and their historical civilisation bears witness, with a strict rule against unverified claims of "miraculous" scientific content; and freedom of belief has an explicit text: "There shall be no compulsion in [acceptance of] the religion", and coerced faith has no value in Allah's scale in the first place.

3. Before You Begin: Prerequisites and Your Place in the Path

This is Unit 11 of 12. It assumes you have passed through Units 1–10, or most of them, for many of its answers build on what came before (divine decree in 8, ethics in 9, women and the family in 10); and whoever comes to it directly will find in every section referrals to the places of detail. Its position, second to last, is deliberate: we do not ask you for a decision (Unit 12) before you have emptied your quiver of all the difficult questions.

4. Unit Objectives

By the end of this unit you will be able to: - Present the violence objection in its strongest forms (including the citation of At-Tawbah 5) and answer it from the context of the complete text and its controls. - Explain Islam's position on the multiplicity of religions and on their followers, doctrinally and humanly, and the historical witness of the dhimmah. - Present the three rules of the answer to the problem of evil, and Islam's disciplined position on modern science without the exaggerations of either side. - Explain the text of freedom of belief and its historical application, and direct your own objection through the "Present Your Objection" channel if anything of it remains.

5. Main Text

5.1 "Does Islam Call to Violence Against Non-Muslims?" — The Unavoidable Question

Let us begin with your hardest question, because it is the question of the whole age, and in the strongest form its proponents state it: "Your Book says: 'then kill the polytheists wherever you find them' [At-Tawbah: 5] — is this not an explicit call to kill dissenters wherever they are found?" A legitimate question, and its answer begins from reading the surah itself, not the verse alone: the verse immediately before it excepts those who were given a covenant and did not break it [At-Tawbah: 4], and the verse immediately after it commands the Prophet ﷺ himself: "And if any one of the polytheists seeks your protection, then grant him protection so that he may hear the words of Allah. Then deliver him to his place of safety" [At-Tawbah: 6] — a polytheist seeks your protection in the very heat of the fighting context, and his right is that you protect him until he hears, then convey him to his place of safety, safe, even if he does not believe. So the context is a declared war with treaty-breaking tribes besieging the emerging state, not an open licence against a dissenter in religion.

And the honest answer is completed from the complete text: the Qur'an that contains verses of fighting is the same one that says: "Allah does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes - from being righteous toward them and acting justly toward them. Indeed, Allah loves those who act justly" [Al-Mumtahanah: 8], and says: "Fight in the way of Allah those who fight against you but do not transgress. Indeed, Allah does not like transgressors" [Al-Baqarah: 190], and makes killing a single soul without right like killing all people: "whoever kills a soul unless for a soul or for corruption [done] in the land - it is as if he had slain mankind entirely. And whoever saves one - it is as if he had saved mankind entirely" [Al-Ma'idah: 32].

What, then, is the context of the fighting verses? They were revealed in an emerging state besieged by tribes and empires that wanted to exterminate it, so it legislated fighting in defence, and fighting those who fight, with controls that the wars of its age did not know: no killing of a woman, a child, an old man or a monk in his hermitage, no cutting of a tree, no mutilation of a corpse, and no betrayal of a covenant — these are the instructions of the Prophet ﷺ and his caliphs to their armies, established in the books of the Sunnah and the biography. And when the Prophet ﷺ was given security over his enemies on the day of the conquest of Makkah, he pardoned them while able to take revenge, as you read in Unit Five. As for the groups that kill civilians in the name of Islam today, the scholars of the Muslims and their major institutions in the world — from Al-Azhar to the fiqh academies — declare their violation of Islam's Sharia and its controls, and that their fighting, in their view, is corruption in the land, not jihad. Judge the complete text by its biography, not by selected clips from news bulletins — the same demand we ask of you for every religion and idea.

And note a method that will recur throughout this whole unit, because it is our announced measure: a single verse is never read alone. Just as it is not valid to take from the constitution of any state a single article on "penalties" and ignore its articles on rights and guarantees and then claim you have described the system, likewise it is not valid to cut a fighting verse out of its surah, its historical context and its controls stated explicitly in other places in the same Book. And we will apply this method before you in every coming question: the objection in its strongest form, then the text in its complete context, then the practical historical witness, then your referral to the source to verify for yourself. If you find us falling short of this method anywhere, remind us of it through the "Present Your Objection" channel at the end of the unit — for it is our covenant with you, not merely our slogan.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Mumtahanah: 8–9], [Al-Baqarah: 190], [Al-Ma'idah: 32], [At-Tawbah: 4–6] on the context of the fighting verses and granting protection to the one who seeks it. - Islam Q&A (islamqa.info) — the "Doubts about jihad and the fighting verses" files (selected and summarised with referral). - Safiur Rahman Mubarakpuri, Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar) — chapters on the instructions of war and the conquest of Makkah. - Islamweb (islamweb.net) — materials on "The controls of fighting in Islam".

5.2 "If the Truth Is One, Why Are There Many Religions? And How Do You View Their Followers?"

A question you asked in Unit One, and here is its wider answer. Islam says: the multiplicity of religions today has two causes: heavenly messages whose origin was truth, then distortion and forgetting entered their texts across the centuries, and human endeavours and philosophies that tried to fill the void of the unseen without revelation. And Allah, in the belief of Muslims, did not will to coerce human beings into one religion by force; rather, He made this world an abode of trial by choice: "And had your Lord willed, those on earth would have believed - all of them entirely. Then, [O Muhammad], would you compel the people in order that they become believers?" [Yunus: 99].

As for looking at the followers of other religions, the Qur'an distinguishes between the doctrinal position and the human position with precise distinction: doctrinally, the Muslim believes his message is the final, preserved one, and he calls others to it with wisdom. And humanly and socially, his religion commands him to be righteous to the peaceful dissenter and just toward him [Al-Mumtahanah: 8], permits him the food of the People of the Book and marriage to their women [Al-Ma'idah: 5], and describes the nearest people in affection to the Muslims as the Christians, because of their priests and monks and their humility: "And you will find the nearest of them in affection to the believers those who say, 'We are Christians.' That is because among them are priests and monks and because they are not arrogant" [Al-Ma'idah: 82]. And in practical history, Jews, Christians, Magians and Sabians lived under the states of Islam for fourteen centuries as citizens whose blood, wealth and churches were preserved by a legal covenant called the dhimmah, and 'Umar ibn al-Khattab wrote for the people of Jerusalem a famous covenant in which he gave them security for themselves, their wealth, their crosses and their churches, that they would not be inhabited or demolished. Ask yourself: how many ancient or modern civilisations granted their dissenters in religion a written covenant of security like this in the seventh century CE?

And the covenant was not merely words on paper; among the fruits of this distinction between the doctrinal and the human is that whole quarters of the followers of other religions remained in the lands of Islam across the centuries, with their churches, synagogues, schools and their own personal-status courts, and that there reached us from their manuscript heritage in the libraries of Eastern monasteries and churches what did not reach us from the heritage of their centuries in other countries that persecuted them. And we do not claim that history was rosy without exceptions — in scattered centuries there occurred transgressions and injustice that Muslim historians themselves recorded and criticised by the measure of their Sharia before anyone else — but the general rule that governed fourteen centuries was covenant and protection, and the exception was denounced as a departure from the rule, not an application of it. And this is an essential difference in fair historical judgement: that a civilisation is reckoned by its announced, binding rule, and its exceptions are held to account by that same rule.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Yunus: 99], [Al-Mumtahanah: 8], [Al-Ma'idah: 5, 82], [Al-Kafirun: 6]. - Islam Q&A — the "A Muslim's relationship with a non-Muslim" and "Ahl adh-Dhimmah" files. - Ibn Kathir, Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim — at the interpretation of Al-Ma'idah 82; and the books of biography and history for 'Umar's covenant to the people of Jerusalem (narrated by the famous books of conquests and history such as At-Tabari and Ibn Kathir in Al-Bidayah wa an-Nihayah).

5.3 "Why Do Evil and Pain Exist If Allah Is Merciful and Able?"

A question renewed with every earthquake, every war and every child in pain. We prepared for it in Unit Three, and here we complete it with three calm rules:

The first: that much of the world's evil is made by human beings themselves — wars, injustice, corruption and neglect — and Allah has charged the human being with responsibility for it because He created him with choice; abolishing human evil by coercion would mean abolishing freedom itself, and a world without freedom is not a world of humans but a world of machines. The second: that some pain is part of the nature of the temporary abode of testing — illness, old age and parting — and its function in the Islamic conception is trial, expiation and elevation in degrees, and its complete reward is in an abode with no pain in it; so the fair comparison is not between "a world with pain" and "a world without pain", but between a story that ends in eternal justice and a story that ends in eternal absurdity. The third: that our ignorance of the complete wisdom is not evidence of its absence; a child cries at the vaccination needle and does not realise it is his life, and the difference between our knowledge and Allah's knowledge is greater than the difference between the child and his doctor, beyond comparison.

And with all of this, Islam does not say to the afflicted: "Be silent, this is your decree", then walk away; rather, it commands him to take the means of healing and salvation, commands his society to console him and help him, and makes patience a worship promised a reward without account: "Indeed, the patient will be given their reward without account" [Az-Zumar: 10]. Indeed, it addresses the afflicted themselves in a direct address that gathers trial and glad tidings in a single breath: "And give good tidings to the patient, Who, when disaster strikes them, say, 'Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return'" [Al-Baqarah: 155–156] — and it repeats to the burdened an unambiguous double promise: "For indeed, with hardship [will be] ease. Indeed, with hardship [will be] ease" [Ash-Sharh: 5–6] — with hardship, not only after it. A scenario: your non-Muslim friend lost his son, and he asks you, weeping: where is your God's mercy? The honest Islamic answer is not a cold lecture, but: Allah's mercy encompassed your son, who was not charged; He made your affliction a path to a great reward if you are patient; and He promised you a meeting after which there is no parting — if you believe. Your friend may not be convinced today, but he heard an answer that respects his pain and does not belittle it.

And note, finally, in this chapter, that Islam does not present its answer to evil as a cold philosophy taught in a hall, but as a practical system that all moves at the first calamity: the sick person is visited, helped and prayed for; the dead person is washed, shrouded, prayed over, buried with dignity and prayed for years afterwards; the orphan is sponsored by texts that make his sponsor a companion of the Prophet ﷺ in Paradise; and the whole society is commanded to console, feed and mend hearts. So when the questioner asks, "Where is Allah in relation to pain?", Islam answers with two complementary answers: the answer of meaning — an abode of trial, reward and wisdom — and the answer of action: a whole society that moves to relieve pain instead of watching it. And many of those who entered Islam through the doors of calamities were not convinced by proof alone; they were convinced that at the first earthquake in their lives they found hands reaching out, hearts present, and a creed that gave their patience a meaning — all three together, not one of them alone.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Baqarah: 155–157], [Az-Zumar: 10], [Ash-Sharh: 5–6]. - Islam Q&A — answers to "Why do evils and calamities exist?" and "The wisdom of trial" for seekers. - Wahiduddin Khan, Islam and the Modern Man / Islam Challenges — chapters on the problem of evil in philosophy and religion.

5.4 "Does Islam Conflict With Modern Science?"

A disciplined answer, without exaggerations from either side: Islam is a religion that makes looking at the universe and reflecting on its signs worship, and commands seeking knowledge absolutely. The first thing by which the Qur'an describes people of intellect is that they reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth: "Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the night and the day are signs for those of understanding" [Aal 'Imran: 190]. And its historical civilisation produced — in the centuries of its strong faith, not in spite of it — pioneers in medicine, astronomy, mathematics and optics from whom Europe transmitted in its renaissance, such as Ibn al-Haytham in optics, Al-Khwarizmi in algebra and Ibn Sina in medicine. So a structural conflict between "Islam" and "science" is a claim to which the history of the Muslims themselves does not testify.

As for the specific questions: the Qur'an, for Muslims, is a book of guidance, not a book of experimental physics; it establishes doctrinal foundations — that Allah is the Creator of everything, and that the human being is an honoured creature with a special origin — and it does not oblige the Muslim to a single reading of the details of the mechanisms of natural creation unless a definitive text states it. And on the subject of evolution specifically, the authoritative position among the majority of contemporary Muslim scholars is: accepting what has been scientifically established of the change and adaptation of creatures within its experimental limits, and rejecting its generalisation as a philosophy that negates the Creator or makes the human being a mere animal extension without special creation or honouring — because that, in their view, is a departure from science into a materialist creed disguised in the garment of science. And we commit on this site to a strict rule: we do not attribute to the Qur'an a "scientific miraculousness" that has not been established among specialists, and we do not present moving scientific hypotheses as final facts; for religion is too firmly established to be tied to theories that change, and science is too honourable to be used as propaganda. Whoever wants to go deeper, the references of this chapter are documented in the section's sources.

And a final alert in this chapter that concerns us before our opponents: materials of "scientific miraculousness" attributed to the Qur'an without verification are spread on the internet — verses whose necks are twisted to match a modern discovery, invented numbers and statistics, and stories of "a scientist who became Muslim after reading a verse" with no origin. This site's position on them is its position on every undocumented claim wherever it appears: rejection and deletion. For the truth we believe in does not need small lies to serve it; and every false claim that is exposed — and it will be exposed — strikes the credibility of the whole call in the eyes of an honest seeker who was about to believe. For this reason you will not find on this whole site a single verse interpreted with a scientific claim not established among specialists, nor a single story of a famous person's Islam without a documented source with his name and his book.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-'Ankabut: 20], [Al-Mulk: 19], [Aal 'Imran: 190–191]. - Wahiduddin Khan, Islam Challenges — chapters on science and religion and the method of comparison. - Islam Q&A — the "Islam and science" and "The theory of evolution" files (presented in the form of the authoritative position with referral, without detailed fatwa). - Dala'il Centre — materials on the relationship between religion and science (adopted after internal religious review, and not transmitted from without verification).

5.5 "What About Freedom of Belief? And How Is the Verse 'No Compulsion in Religion' to Be Read?"

The text is explicit and needs no contrived interpretation: "There shall be no compulsion in [acceptance of] the religion. The right course has become clear from the wrong" [Al-Baqarah: 256]. And the Meccan Qur'an sealed one of its addresses with this same freedom of choice: "so whoever wills - let him believe; and whoever wills - let him disbelieve" [Al-Kahf: 29]. For faith, among Muslims, is a heartfelt affirmation that is not valid by coercion in the first place; because a coerced hypocrite is not a believer in Allah's scale, so the religion has no interest in coercing anyone. For this reason Allah addressed His Prophet himself: "So remind, [O Muhammad]; you are only a reminder. You are not over them a controller" [Al-Ghashiyah: 21–22]; your function is reminder and conveyance, not control over hearts.

And practically: the history of the Islamic conquests did not know — by the testimony of fair historians who are not Muslims — courts of inquisition forcing populations to change their religion; and churches and temples remained standing in Egypt, the Levant and Al-Andalus for long centuries under Islamic rule, and the people of those lands entered Islam gradually across generations through conviction and mixing, not by the edge of the sword — for had it been by the sword, it would have been completed in a single generation. As for the detailed jurisprudential rulings relating to particular historical contexts (such as the rulings on apostasy in the old state and their relation to armed political treason in their age), their place is the books of fiqh and the specialised objection files referred to below, and we present them there in their complete contexts, not selected ones; for our site hides no question and does not simplify harmfully.

A final question returns the ball to you, fairly: you are reading this unit now on a da'wah site that presents a whole religion to you with argument and gentleness, and leaves you your decision from the first unit. If this religion were a religion of coercion in its origin, would it have needed a site like this in the first place?

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Baqarah: 256], [Al-Ghashiyah: 21–22], [Yunus: 99], [Al-Kahf: 29]. - Islam Q&A — the "No compulsion in religion" and "Freedom of belief in Islam" files (selected and summarised with referral). - Ibn Kathir, Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim — at the interpretation of Al-Baqarah 256 and the reason for its revelation. - Dorar Al-Saniyyah (dorar.net) — to verify any hadith appearing in this chapter before publication.

5.6 Small Practical Questions Asked Every Day: Pork, Wine and the Etiquette of Meeting

We conclude with questions lighter in weight and more frequent in daily life, because the seeker lives among Muslims before he reads books about them:

Why does a Muslim not eat pork? Because Allah forbade it in an explicit text in the Qur'an [Al-Baqarah: 173], so the Muslim abstains in worship and obedience first, whether a health wisdom appears to him or not; for obedience, for the believer, is not conditional on understanding the reason in every command. And why does he not drink wine? Because Allah forbade it and described it as defilement from the work of Satan that causes enmity and hatred and averts from the remembrance of Allah [Al-Ma'idah: 90–91] — causes that everyone who has witnessed the effects of addiction in families, roads and hospitals sees today, and countries and global health organisations have begun to reconsider their leniency toward alcohol because of its documented harms. And why do some Muslims shake hands, while others refrain from shaking hands with the opposite sex? Practical jurisprudential matters whose details are in trusted fatwa references, and what is required of you as a colleague or friend is one simple thing: do not embarrass and do not be embarrassed; smile and content yourself with a kind verbal greeting if you see a hand that does not extend, for most likely it is respect, not contempt.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Baqarah: 173], [Al-Ma'idah: 90–91]. - Islam Q&A — answers to "The wisdom of forbidding pork" and "The wisdom of forbidding wine" for seekers. - Islamweb — materials introducing colleagues and friends practically to Muslims' customs.

5.7 How Do You Verify for Yourself? A Method for Examining Any Claim About Islam in Five Steps

We conclude the unit with a methodological gift that will serve you after us with any source — with us before others: when you hear a claim about Islam, from us or from its opponents, examine it with these five steps before you build a position on it:

The first: Demand the text itself, not its description. Ninety percent of shocking claims collapse when the verse or hadith is read completely from its source, not from a screenshot in a post. The second: Read the two contexts: the context of the text within its surah and its place, and its historical context in which it was revealed or said; you saw in the first section how context alone overturned the meaning of a whole verse. The third: Ask about the degree of documentation: is this Qur'an, or an authenticated, referenced hadith, or a weak hadith transmitted by preachers, or a historical story disputed in its establishment? Each rank has its weight, and this site commits to stating the rank to you in every material. The fourth: Compare text and practice and do not confuse them: the practice of a Muslim, a state or a group is not an argument against the text, nor an argument for it, except to the extent of its agreement with it — the measure by which we judged, in the women's unit, the practices of whole countries. The fifth: Hear from both sides before judgement: from trusted specialists on the side you criticise, not from its opponents alone nor its supporters alone; for the fairness you demand from others for yourself, demand it from yourself for others.

Apply these steps to this unit itself first: open the sources referred to, compare our answers with their origins, and note what is not established for you. For the reader who examines us with this method is exactly the reader for whom this whole project was designed. And with this same method we ask that our coming final unit be read: it will not ask you for a decision built on a moment's emotion, but on the outcome of all this examination of yours — eleven open files with their sources, five major objections in their strongest forms and their answers, and a method of verification that is now with you for the rest of your life, for any claim you hear about any religion or idea. If your examination ends with the conclusion that this religion is truth, then the last door is open and its step is simpler than you think; and if it ends otherwise, you have gained at least an accurate, documented picture instead of a distorted, inherited one — and that alone is a gain not to be underestimated for a fair reader. So proceed to the final unit with an open heart and a mind armed with the method, and know that no one in this whole project will celebrate a hasty decision, nor was it built to manufacture one; our real celebration — as you will see — is only for a free, mature decision that came out of a complete examination like this one you have accomplished yourself — so cut the last rung of this path slowly, for although it is the shortest of its units in pages, it is the greatest of them in effect over a whole lifetime.
