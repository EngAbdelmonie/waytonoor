1. Opening Hook

Before he was a prophet, his very opponents used to call him by one title for which they knew no other: "the Truthful, the Trustworthy" (As-Sadiq Al-Amin). They would entrust him with their money and their secrets while they were not of his religion. So what happened when he came to these very people with the message? And why did the situation turn into enmity, pursuit and expulsion? The story of this man is not told from its end; it is told from its beginning — and this unit tells it to you from its documented sources, situation by situation.

2. Executive Summary

Muhammad ibn Abdullah ﷺ, a poor orphan raised in Makkah, was known by his people for forty years for truthfulness and trustworthiness before he claimed anything. At forty, revelation came to him, and he spent thirteen years in Makkah patient with harm, with no authority and no army; then he emigrated and built in Madinah a community and a charter of shared life, and he sealed his practical biography with a general pardon of the people of Makkah on the day he had power over them. For his truthfulness, the Muslims present a bundle of proofs: his biography before prophethood and after it; the nature of the Book he brought while he was unlettered; and the Qur'an's rebuke of him in places that an author would not immortalise in his own book. Among his followers he is a human messenger, loved and followed but not worshipped; he died as human beings die, and his message remained after him.

3. Before You Begin: Prerequisites and Your Place in the Path

This is Unit 5 of 12. It presupposes Unit Four, "The Prophets and Messengers," which drew the whole chain and its seal; this unit opens the file of the Seal himself. It stands on its own for whoever reached it directly, but reading it after the fourth makes its question more precise: not only "Who is Muhammad?", but "Do you believe this man's claim that he is the seal of that chain?"

4. Unit Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to: - Narrate the major stations of the biography of Muhammad ﷺ before prophethood and after it, in their chronological order. - Explain the proofs of truthfulness that the Muslims present, as their presenters set them out. - Describe his character ﷺ in his home and among people, from the testimonies of those who lived with him. - Distinguish precisely between the Prophet's place ﷺ among the Muslims (a human messenger who is followed as an example) and worship, which belongs to Allah alone.

5. Main Text

5.1 Who Is Muhammad? A Glimpse of His Life Before the Message

Muhammad ibn Abdullah was born in Makkah around the year 570 CE, fatherless, then lost his mother while he was a small child, so he grew up in the care of his grandfather, then of his uncle Abu Talib. The Qur'an itself summed up this upbringing in a direct address to him: ﴿Did He not find you an orphan and give [you] refuge? And He found you lost and guided [you]. And He found you poor and made [you] self-sufficient﴾ [Ad-Duha: 6–8] — an orphan, and He sheltered him; searching and astray, and He guided him; and poor, and He enriched him by His favour. He was not from a house of kingship or wealth; he grew up poor, herding sheep and then working in trade, until all of Quraysh knew him by one title by which no one else in his society was known: "the Truthful, the Trustworthy" (As-Sadiq Al-Amin). This testimony is very important in the scale of fair evaluation, for it did not come from his followers after his call succeeded, but from his people before prophethood, when they would entrust him with their money and their deposits while knowing that he neither lies nor betrays.

At twenty-five he married Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, may Allah be pleased with her, a merchant woman of wealth and honour, and he lived with her for twenty-five years a faithful husband who did not marry another alongside her, until she died while she was the most beloved of people to him. He ﷺ was known in his society for maintaining ties of kinship, bearing others' burdens, honouring the guest and helping the wronged, far from the worship of idols and from the gatherings of amusement and wine that were widespread around him; Allah had protected him from the filth of the pre-Islamic ignorance since his upbringing. And when he reached forty, while he was worshipping in the cave of Hira, reflecting on the creation of the heavens and the earth, the first revelation came to him with a word that became the title of his whole message: ﴿Recite in the name of your Lord who created﴾ [Al-'Alaq: 1].

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-'Alaq: 1], [Ad-Duha: 6–8]. - Safiur Rahman Mubarakpuri, Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar) — the chapters on lineage, birth, upbringing and his mission ﷺ. - IslamWeb website (islamweb.net) — the Prophetic Biography Encyclopaedia.

5.2 What Happened After the Revelation? Thirteen Years of Patience in Makkah

Muhammad ﷺ did not receive the message with ready authority, nor with an army, nor with money, but with a call, secret then public, in a city that worshipped idols and lived off the pilgrimage seasons to them. The recompense was thirteen years of mockery, boycott and torture of his first followers, until he ﷺ was driven out of the city in which he was born, and emigrated to Madinah in the year 622 CE — the year with which the Hijri calendar begins. There he built the first Muslim community: a mosque gathering the people, and a written charter organising shared life between the Muslims, the Jews and the tribes of Madinah on the basis of shared citizenship and shared defence — a document historians count among the oldest texts organising religious plurality within a single society.

Then came the years of confrontation with Quraysh, who wanted to extinguish the call by force, so the wars were, for the most part, in defence of a besieged, emerging community. They ended with the Treaty of Al-Hudaybiyyah, then with the Conquest of Makkah in the eighth year of the Hijrah — a conquest in which bloodshed was almost entirely absent: the Prophet ﷺ entered the city that had harmed him and expelled him, and said to its people what Joseph, peace be upon him, had said to his brothers before: ﴿He said, "No blame will there be upon you today. May Allah forgive you; and He is the most merciful of the merciful"﴾ [Yusuf: 92], and he declared a general pardon for them. This stance alone — pardon at the moment of complete power — is among the strongest of what the Muslims present as a practical witness to the nature of the man and his message: ﴿And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds﴾ [Al-Anbiya: 107].

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Anbiya: 107], [Yusuf: 92]. - Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar) — the chapters on the Makkan call, the Hijrah, the Charter of Madinah and the Conquest of Makkah. - Sahih al-Bukhari — the Book of Military Expeditions (the chapters on the Treaty of Al-Hudaybiyyah and the Conquest of Makkah), verified via Dorar Al-Sunnah (dorar.net). - Muhammad Husayn Haykal, Hayat Muhammad (The Life of Muhammad) — for an accessible historical presentation of the stages of the call.

5.3 What Are the Proofs of His Truthfulness That the Muslims Present?

A legitimate question that does not embarrass us: why do the Muslims believe that Muhammad ﷺ is a messenger from Allah, and not a righteous man who invented a message? The Muslims present a bundle of overlapping proofs, not a single proof, the most important of them in brief:

First: his biography before prophethood and after it. Lying about Allah is the greatest possible lie, and it is impossible, rationally and by custom, for a man to live forty years attested for absolute truthfulness, then suddenly invent a lie for which he lives twenty-three years of hardship and persecution, while he is able at every moment to compromise and be rich. Second: the nature of the message itself. An unlettered man who neither reads nor writes came with a Book that the eloquent men of his age were unable to rival, carrying legislation, morality, reports and a complete conception of life, and this Book remained preserved letter by letter for fourteen centuries. The Qur'an itself stated this unletteredness and made it part of the proof: ﴿And you did not recite before it any scripture, nor did you inscribe one with your right hand. Then [i.e., otherwise] the falsifiers would have had [cause for] doubt﴾ [Al-'Ankabut: 48] — if you had been a reader and a writer before the revelation, the doubters would have had an opening; but since you neither read nor write, from where did this Book come? Third: his honesty with what does not benefit him. In the Qur'an there is an explicit rebuke of the Prophet ﷺ in specific situations — such as his turning away from the blind learner and occupying himself with the leaders of the people ['Abasa: 1–10] — and if he were the author of the Book, he would not have immortalised in it a rebuke of himself. Fourth: the fulfilment of what he reported of future matters in his life and after it, as the documented books of the biography relate — at their head, the promise that the Romans would prevail after their defeat within a few years [Ar-Rum: 2–4].

We present these proofs as their presenters present them, and we leave their weighing to you; for our method in this whole path is to present the claim and its proof, and you are the owner of the judgement.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: ['Abasa: 1–10], [Ar-Rum: 2–4], [Al-'Ankabut: 48]. - Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar) — the chapters on the signs of prophethood and his life ﷺ before the mission. - Islam Question & Answer website (islamqa.info) — answers on "What Is the Proof of the Truthfulness of Muhammad ﷺ?" for seekers.

5.4 Muhammad in His Home and Among People: The Character Seen by Those Who Lived With Him

The truest picture of the man is not taken from his public sermons, but from the testimony of those who lived with him in his home and served him in his travel and his presence. His wife Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, was asked about his character, and she said a word that became a rule: "His character was the Qur'an" — narrated by Muslim (746); meaning that he was the living, practical translation of everything his Book calls to. And his servant Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "I served the Prophet ﷺ for ten years, and he never said to me 'uff' at all, and he never said to me about a thing I had done, 'Why did you do this?', nor about a thing I had left, 'Why did you leave this?'" — narrated by Al-Bukhari (6038) and Muslim (2309). Ten years of daily service without a single word of rebuke from a master to a young servant — in an age in which servants were young and had almost no rights.

And he ﷺ would mend his own sandal, patch his garment, and be at work in his family's service in his home; he would visit the sick, answer the invitation of the poor, walk in the need of the widow and the destitute, and play with children and greet them. And in war — where men's mettle appears — he forbade the killing of women, children, the elderly and monks in their hermitages, and the cutting of fruit-bearing trees and the ruining of crops, and he fulfilled his covenants even with his enemies, until his Qur'an said of him: ﴿And indeed, you are of a great moral character﴾ [Al-Qalam: 4]. He was the most generous of people, the bravest of them and the most forbearing; he was never asked for anything and said "No," and he never took revenge for himself unless the sanctities of Allah were violated.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Al-Qalam: 4], [Al-Ahzab: 21]. - Sahih Muslim (746) — "His character was the Qur'an"; and Sahih al-Bukhari (6038) and Sahih Muslim (2309) — the hadith of Anas on his service ﷺ — verification via Dorar Al-Sunnah (dorar.net). - At-Tirmidhi, Ash-Shamail Al-Muhammadiyyah — the chapters on his character, his humility and his way of life ﷺ.

5.5 What Did Fair-Minded Non-Muslims Say About Him?

We do not need outside testimonies to prove what we believe, but it is a matter of fairness that you know the calm picture of Muhammad ﷺ is not the opinion of the Muslims alone. The American author Michael Hart placed him, in his book The 100: A Ranking of the Most Influential Persons in History (1978), in first place, and explained that he was the only man in history who succeeded supremely on both the religious and the worldly levels together, although he began as a poor orphan in a desert environment far from the centres of civilisation. And many Western encyclopaedias and history books have transmitted, from fair-minded historians and Orientalists, their acknowledgement of the greatness of his effect and the historical truthfulness of his call — we mention them here by the names of their authors and their books so that you can return to them yourself, and we transmit from them only what is established in their documented publications, because the method of this site is that the call to truth is not built on fabricated testimonies or ones stripped of context.

It is enough, at the end of this section, to point to a striking paradox: the man who is drawn in some media in offensive images is the same man followed today by about two billion human beings, in love and voluntary imitation, and after whom millions of newborns are named every year on every continent. So which is more worthy of drawing your picture of him: the drawings of his opponents, or the documented books of his biography and the lives of those who knew him?

Sources: - Michael H. 5.6 A Human Being Who Dies, and a Message That Remains

In the eleventh year of the Hijrah, the Prophet ﷺ fell ill with his final illness, and he died in Madinah at the age of sixty-three, while the Qur'an itself had addressed him with this truth beforehand: ﴿Indeed, you are to die, and indeed, they are to die﴾ [Az-Zumar: 30]. His companions were shaken with a great shaking, until Abu Bakr As-Siddiq, may Allah be pleased with him, stood and recited to them His words, the Exalted: ﴿Muhammad is not but a messenger. [Other] messengers have passed on before him. So if he was to die or be killed, would you turn back on your heels [to unbelief]?﴾ [Aal-e-Imran: 144]. And he said his immortal words: whoever used to worship Muhammad, then Muhammad has died; and whoever used to worship Allah, then Allah is Ever-Living and does not die.

In this decisive moment appears the essential difference with which we want you to leave this unit: Muhammad ﷺ among the Muslims is a human being and a messenger — loved, followed as an example, and prayed upon — but he is not worshipped, not called upon besides Allah, and not raised above his humanness. Whoever of the Muslims you see exaggerating about him with an exaggeration that takes him out of being a servant of Allah and His messenger has contradicted the explicit Qur'an of the man himself and his Sunnah. Islam is a religion of pure monotheism: the messenger pointed to the door, and the door is Allah alone.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Aal-e-Imran: 144], [Az-Zumar: 30]. - Sahih al-Bukhari (4454) — the address of Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, on the day of the Prophet's death ﷺ, via Dorar Al-Sunnah for verification. - Ar-Raheeq Al-Makhtum (The Sealed Nectar) — the chapters on the Prophet's illness and death ﷺ and the Highest Companion.

5.7 The Signs of Prophethood: How Do the Muslims Weigh Their Prophet's Truthfulness?

A fair mind will ask: very well, this is his biography and his character; so what is the proof that he is a messenger from Allah and not merely a great man? The Muslims have an accumulated file of signs, which they present ordered from the nearest to the furthest. The first of them is his biography itself, before the call and after it — forty years of attested truthfulness until his people nicknamed him "Al-Amin" (the Trustworthy) — then a call in which he sought neither money, nor kingship, nor women, and steadfastness in it under siege, threat and temptation together; for the lying fabricator — in the scale of reason — seeks worldly gain by his claim, and this man was offered the whole world to stop, and he refused. The second is his character in strength, not in weakness only: many people are humble while they are in need, but to pardon while able to take revenge — as he did on the day of the Conquest of Makkah, saying to Quraysh what Joseph said to his brothers: ﴿"No blame will there be upon you today"﴾ [Yusuf: 92] — that is a character that cannot be acted for twenty-three connected years before enemies lying in wait for every slip of his. The third is his truthful reports of unseen matters that were fulfilled as he reported, and Allah's preservation of his Book and his biography to a degree of documentation unmatched in the biographies of the ancients, until his life ﷺ became the most documented life of ancient history with a connected chain of narration — you eat as he ate, and sleep as he slept.

And the fourth — which among them is the remaining, ever-renewed proof — is the Qur'an itself that he brought: an unlettered man who neither reads nor writes brings a Book that challenged the eloquent of his age to bring its like, and they were unable; and the challenge remained standing and the Book preserved, as you will read in the next unit. And the Muslims do not ask you to accept this file all at once without examination; on the contrary: the method of this whole path is that you present every proof to your reason and your heart together, and that you ask, compare and discuss. For the call that fears examination is not this call.

And pause at the first proof for a little, because on its own it is a complete lesson in the logic of truthfulness: when Muhammad ﷺ began his call, the first thing with which he faced his people was a question about his biography among them: I have lived among you a lifetime before this — forty complete years in which you know my truthfulness and my trustworthiness, until you named me Al-Amin — so is it conceivable that I would lie to people for forty years and then lie about Allah in the greatest of all reports? This argument from the prior biography is the very one the Qur'an advances in His words: ﴿Say, "If Allah had willed, I would not have recited it to you, nor would He have made it known to you, for I had remained among you a lifetime before it. Then will you not reason?"﴾ [Yunus: 16]. The proof among the Muslims is not "a miracle that is related" only, but a biography that is examined: forty years of daily observation by an intelligent people keen to deny him afterwards, and they found in his archive not a single lie to rest upon, and were forced to accuse him of magic, madness and poetry — accusations that contradict one another, all of them an implicit confession that the man is truthful in reason and trustworthiness, and that they are incapable of explaining what he brought except by fleeing from the word "messenger." When the fair researcher weighs this whole archive, he understands why the Muslims present "the man's biography" as the first of their proofs, not the last of them.

Sources: - The Noble Qur'an: [Yusuf: 92], [Al-'Ankabut: 48] — a reference to the texts of sections 5.2 and 5.3; and [Yunus: 16] on the Prophet's argument ﷺ from his biography before the call. - IslamWeb website (islamweb.net) — introductory entries on "The Signs of the Prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ." - Islam Question & Answer website (islamqa.info) — answers on "The Proofs of the Prophet's Truthfulness ﷺ" for seekers.

5.8 A Human Being Who Dies, and With Him the Chain Is Completed: A Final Word in This Unit

We closed Unit Five where the biography itself closed: with the death of the Prophet ﷺ in Madinah in the year 632 CE among his companions — and you read in section 5.6 His words, the Exalted: ﴿Indeed, you are to die, and indeed, they are to die﴾ [Az-Zumar: 30]. And pause here at a paradox not usually found in the stories of founders: the man who changed the history of humanity left his family no inheritance of money, did not appoint a successor from his descendants to inherit his "spiritual office," and no church was built around his grave to be run in his name; he left a preserved Book, a documented Sunnah, and a nation carrying the message after him. For the message among the Muslims is greater than the messenger himself — and he ﷺ was the first to teach them that, when he said of himself that he is the servant of Allah and His messenger, and forbade being praised in the exaggerated way in which other prophets before him had been praised. That is why the nation did not collapse at his death with the collapse of movements tied to persons; rather, the message continued, because it never was "a man's project" but preserved revelation which the nation undertook to carry, generation after generation. For this very reason, whoever measures the Muslims' relationship with their Prophet by the relationship of the followers of founders in other traditions is mistaken: the Muslim does not worship Muhammad ﷺ, does not pray to him, and does not ask him for intercession as an act of worship; and there is in Islam no "Muhammadan church" running the religion in his name. There is a nation carrying a Book and a Sunnah, and honouring the man who conveyed them both with the honour of the pupil for his teacher, the soldier for his commander, and the son for his spiritual father — love and following, not worship and deification. And between love and deification there is a line the Prophet ﷺ himself drew with strictness, and the Muslims guarded after him; whenever you see a Muslim exaggerating until he almost crosses this line, you find the scholars of his nation the first to return him to it. This precision about the man's place in his religion is itself among the strongest witnesses that the project was not the project of a person building his own glory; for founders seeking glory do not draw limits to their own sanctification by their own hands. Keep this picture, then, as you fold up the unit: a man of flesh and blood, who began an orphan and died relatively poor, as he had lived, but who left behind him a preserved Book, a nation carrying his message, and a character that changed the face of the earth — and his biography is open before you in all its details, for you to weigh it yourself.

So if you gather the units of the path so far: One Merciful God (2), a noble purpose for existence (3), a chain of messengers with one message (4), and a Seal who is human, of documented biography and character (5) — there remains the next natural question: and the message itself, the Book he brought — did it reach us as it was actually revealed? It is an entirely legitimate question, and its answer is a complete file standing on its own, of proofs, manuscripts and mass transmission.
